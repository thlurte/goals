### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Convex Optimization* — Stephen Boyd & Lieven Vandenberghe
* **Sections**: §11.4–11.5 (*The Barrier Method, Outer Centering Iterations & Complexity*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Logarithmic Barrier Method Algorithm**:
     * Given strictly feasible $x_0$, initial $t^{(0)} > 0$, barrier multiplier $\mu > 1$, and tolerance $\epsilon > 0$:
       1. **Centering Step**: Compute $x^*(t) = \arg\min_x \left( t f_0(x) + \phi(x) \right)$ subject to $A x = b$ using Newton's method.
       2. **Update**: $x \leftarrow x^*(t)$.
       3. **Stopping Criterion**: Terminate if $\frac{m}{t} < \epsilon$.
       4. **Increase Barrier Parameter**: $t \leftarrow \mu t$.
  2. **Outer Iteration Complexity Theorem**:
     $$N = \left\lceil \frac{\ln(m / (t^{(0)}\epsilon))}{\ln \mu} \right\rceil \text{ outer centering steps}$$
  3. **Self-Concordance & Inner Newton Steps**:
     * For self-concordant barriers, the total number of inner Newton steps across all outer iterations is bounded by:
       $$\text{Total Newton Steps} \le \mathcal{O}\left( \sqrt{m} \ln\left(\frac{m}{\epsilon}\right) \right)$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the outer iteration count $N = \lceil \frac{\ln(m / (t^{(0)}\epsilon))}{\ln \mu} \rceil$ from the geometric duality gap decay $\Delta^{(k)} = \frac{m}{t^{(k)}} = \frac{m}{t^{(0)}\mu^k} \le \epsilon$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that for Linear Programs ($f_0(x) = c^T x, f_i(x) = a_i^T x - b_i$), the logarithmic barrier $\phi(x) = -\sum_{i=1}^m \ln(b_i - a_i^T x)$ is standard self-concordant.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For an optimal vector bit allocation program with $m = 128$ inequality constraints, initial $t^{(0)} = 1$, scaling factor $\mu = 10$, and error threshold $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$, compute the exact number of outer centering iterations required ($N = 9$).

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Convex Optimization* — Stephen Boyd & Lieven Vandenberghe
* **Sections**: §11.7–11.8 (*Primal-Dual Interior-Point Methods & Search Directions*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Modified KKT Perturbed Residuals**:
     * For current point $(x, \lambda, \nu)$ with $f_i(x) < 0, \lambda \succ 0$:
       $$r(x, \lambda, \nu) = \begin{bmatrix} r_{\text{dual}} \\ r_{\text{cent}} \\ r_{\text{pri}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \nabla f_0(x) + D f(x)^T \lambda + A^T \nu \\ -\operatorname{diag}(\lambda) f(x) - (1/t)\mathbf{1} \\ A x - b \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{0}$$
  2. **The Primal-Dual Search Direction System**:
     $$\begin{bmatrix} \nabla^2 f_0(x) + \sum \lambda_i \nabla^2 f_i(x) & D f(x)^T & A^T \\ -\operatorname{diag}(\lambda) D f(x) & -\operatorname{diag}(f(x)) & 0 \\ A & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \Delta x_{\text{pd}} \\ \Delta \lambda_{\text{pd}} \\ \Delta \nu_{\text{pd}} \end{bmatrix} = -\begin{bmatrix} r_{\text{dual}} \\ r_{\text{cent}} \\ r_{\text{pri}} \end{bmatrix}$$
  3. **Surrogate Duality Gap & Adaptive Centering**:
     * Surrogate duality gap: $\hat{\eta}(x, \lambda) = -\sum_{i=1}^m \lambda_i f_i(x)$.
     * Set target parameter adaptively: $t = \frac{\mu m}{\hat{\eta}}$ with centering parameter $\mu \approx 10$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the elimination of $\Delta \lambda$: Show that $\Delta \lambda = \operatorname{diag}(1/f(x)) \left( -r_{\text{cent}} + \operatorname{diag}(\lambda) D f(x) \Delta x \right)$, reducing the system to a symmetric positive definite system for $(\Delta x, \Delta \nu)$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that the maximum feasible step size preserving dual positivity $\lambda + \alpha \Delta \lambda \succ 0$ is $\alpha_{\max} = \min \left\{ 1, \; \min_{i: \Delta \lambda_i < 0} \left( -\frac{\lambda_i}{\Delta \lambda_i} \right) \right\}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: On a 2D constrained program $\min \frac{1}{2}(x_1^2 + x_2^2) \text{ s.t. } x_1 + x_2 \ge 2$, compute the exact residuals $r_{\text{dual}}, r_{\text{cent}}, r_{\text{pri}}$ at point $x = [2, 2]^T, \lambda = 1, \nu = 0, t = 10$.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Convex Optimization* — Stephen Boyd & Lieven Vandenberghe
* **Sections**: §11.8 (*Primal-Dual Line Search*) & Discrete Fourier Analysis (*Circulant Matrices & Convolutions*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Primal-Dual Line Search & Residual Descent**:
     * Maximum step size preserving $\lambda \succ 0$: $\alpha_{\max} = \min \left\{ 1, \; \min_{i: \Delta \lambda_i < 0} \left( -\frac{\lambda_i}{\Delta \lambda_i} \right) \right\}$.
     * Backtrack $\alpha \leftarrow \beta \alpha$ until:
       $$\|r(x + \alpha \Delta x, \lambda + \alpha \Delta \lambda, \nu + \alpha \Delta \nu)\|_2 \le (1 - \gamma \alpha) \|r(x, \lambda, \nu)\|_2$$
     * **Descent Property**: The primal-dual direction satisfies $\left. \frac{d}{d\alpha} \|r\|_2^2 \right|_{\alpha=0} = -2 \|r\|_2^2 < 0$.
  2. **Circulant Matrix Diagonalization Theorem**:
     * Circular convolution $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{h} * \mathbf{x} \iff \mathbf{y} = C(\mathbf{h})\mathbf{x}$.
     * Every circulant matrix $C(\mathbf{h})$ is diagonalized by the Discrete Fourier Transform matrix $F$:
       $$C(\mathbf{h}) = F^{-1} \operatorname{diag}(F \mathbf{h}) F$$
     * Transforms $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ matrix-vector products into $\mathcal{O}(n \log n)$ point-wise multiplications in the frequency domain.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove the Circulant Diagonalization Theorem: Show that DFT basis vectors $\mathbf{v}_k = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}[1, \omega^k, \dots, \omega^{(n-1)k}]^T$ ($\omega = e^{-i 2\pi/n}$) are exact eigenvectors of $C(\mathbf{h})$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_k = (F \mathbf{h})_k$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that the derivative of the squared residual norm along the primal-dual direction is strictly negative: $\nabla \|r\|_2^2 \cdot \Delta = -2 r^T r = -2 \|r\|_2^2$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For vector $\mathbf{h} = [2, 1, 0, 1]^T$, write down the $4 \times 4$ circulant matrix $C(\mathbf{h})$ and compute all 4 eigenvalues $\lambda \in \{4, 2, 0, 2\}$ using the 4-point DFT.
