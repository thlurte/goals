### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Convex Optimization* — Stephen Boyd & Lieven Vandenberghe
* **Sections**: §9.1–9.3 (*Unconstrained Minimization, Descent Methods & Strong Convexity*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Strong Convexity & Hessian Bounds**:
     * $f$ is strongly convex on $S$ if $\exists m > 0$ such that $\nabla^2 f(x) \succeq m I$ for all $x \in S$.
     * Upper bound on curvature: $\exists M \ge m$ such that $\nabla^2 f(x) \preceq M I$.
     * Condition Number of sublevel sets: $\kappa = \frac{M}{m} \ge 1$.
  2. **Suboptimality Bound Theorem**:
     $$f(x) - p^* \le \frac{1}{2m} \|\nabla f(x)\|_2^2$$
     * **Derivation**: Strong convexity gives $f(y) \ge f(x) + \nabla f(x)^T (y - x) + \frac{m}{2} \|y - x\|_2^2$. Minimizing over $y$ yields $\tilde{y} = x - \frac{1}{m}\nabla f(x)$, giving $p^* \ge f(x) - \frac{1}{2m}\|\nabla f(x)\|_2^2$.
  3. **Mathematical Model of Filtered Graph Recall Collapse**:
     * In a regular graph of degree $d$, under independent Bernoulli filter selectivity $\sigma = P(\text{pass})$, the probability that a node has zero valid outgoing edges is:
       $$P(\text{Isolated}) = (1 - \sigma)^d$$
     * For $d = 32$ and $\sigma = 0.01$ ($1\%$), $P(\text{Isolated}) = (0.99)^{32} \approx 72.5\%$, proving why post-filtering graph search terminates prematurely.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the suboptimality bound $f(x) - p^* \le \frac{1}{2m}\|\nabla f(x)\|_2^2$ step-by-step from the strong convexity quadratic lower bound.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that the condition number $\kappa = M/m$ bounds the ratio of the major to minor axes of the sublevel set ellipsoids: $\sqrt{M/m}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For an HNSW graph with degree $d = 32$, compute the probability that a random node has at least one valid neighbor $1 - (1-\sigma)^{32}$ across selectivity values $\sigma \in \{0.50, 0.10, 0.01, 0.001\}$, demonstrating the threshold where post-filtering collapses.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Convex Optimization* — Stephen Boyd & Lieven Vandenberghe
* **Sections**: §9.3–9.4 (*Gradient Descent Convergence & Armijo Backtracking Line Search*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Gradient Descent Linear Convergence**:
     * For strongly convex $f$ with $m I \preceq \nabla^2 f(x) \preceq M I$, gradient descent with exact line search satisfies:
       $$f(x^{(k)}) - p^* \le \left( 1 - \frac{m}{M} \right)^k \left( f(x^{(0)}) - p^* \right) = \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\kappa} \right)^k \left( f(x^{(0)}) - p^* \right)$$
     * Number of iterations to reach error $\epsilon$: $K = \mathcal{O}\left( \kappa \ln(1/\epsilon) \right)$.
  2. **Armijo Backtracking Line Search**:
     * Starting at $t = 1$, iteratively scale $t \leftarrow \beta t$ ($\beta \in (0, 1)$) until:
       $$f(x + t \Delta x) \le f(x) + \alpha t \nabla f(x)^T \Delta x \quad (\alpha \in (0, 0.5))$$
     * Guaranteed termination with step size $t \ge \min(1, \beta / M)$.
  3. **ACORN $N$-Hop Connectivity Theory**:
     * In an induced subgraph under filter selectivity $\sigma$, 1-hop edges are severed with probability $1 - \sigma$.
     * Building edges over $N$-hop neighborhoods ($N=2$) expands the effective neighbor pool from $d$ to $\approx d^2 / 2$, ensuring the induced subgraph remains fully connected with high probability:
       $$P(\text{Graph Disconnected}) \le N_{\text{nodes}} \cdot (1 - \sigma)^{d_{\text{2-hop}}}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the gradient descent convergence rate $f(x^+) - p^* \le (1 - m/M)(f(x) - p^*)$: use $f(x - t \nabla f(x)) \le f(x) - t \|\nabla f(x)\|_2^2 + \frac{M t^2}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|_2^2$, minimize over $t = 1/M$ to get $f(x^+) \le f(x) - \frac{1}{2M}\|\nabla f(x)\|_2^2$, and apply the suboptimality bound $\|\nabla f(x)\|_2^2 \ge 2m(f(x) - p^*)$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that Armijo backtracking step size satisfies $t \ge \min(1, \beta/M)$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: On a vector graph with 2-hop neighborhood expansion ($d_{\text{2-hop}} = 128$), compute the probability of a node being isolated at $\sigma = 0.01$ ($1\%$ selectivity): $(0.99)^{128} \approx 0.276$ (down from $0.725$ for $d=32$), demonstrating the restoration of graph connectivity.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Convex Optimization* — Stephen Boyd & Lieven Vandenberghe
* **Sections**: §9.5–9.7 (*Newton's Method, The Newton Decrement & Affine Invariance*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Newton Step & Decrement**:
     * Newton Step: $\Delta x_{\text{nt}} = -(\nabla^2 f(x))^{-1} \nabla f(x)$.
     * Newton Decrement:
       $$\lambda(x) = \left( \nabla f(x)^T (\nabla^2 f(x))^{-1} \nabla f(x) \right)^{1/2} = \|\nabla f(x)\|_{\nabla^2 f(x)^{-1}}$$
     * **Suboptimality Bound**: $f(x) - p^* \le \frac{1}{2}\lambda(x)^2$, yielding the universal stopping criterion $\frac{1}{2}\lambda(x)^2 \le \epsilon$.
  2. **Affine Invariance Theorem**:
     * Let $y = T x$ ($T$ invertible) and define $\bar{f}(y) = f(T^{-1}y)$.
     * The Newton step transforms contravariantly: $\Delta y_{\text{nt}} = T \Delta x_{\text{nt}}$.
     * The Newton decrement is strictly invariant: $\bar{\lambda}(y) = \lambda(x)$.
     * (Newton's method is immune to coordinate scaling and ill-conditioning).
  3. **Amortized Graph Deletion Theory**:
     * Immediate edge repair upon deletion costs $\mathcal{O}(M^2)$ distance evaluations.
     * Soft-deletion via atomic tombstone bitset costs $\mathcal{O}(1)$.
     * Periodic background vacuuming at tombstone threshold $\tau = 10\%$ restores optimal graph connectivity with amortized $\mathcal{O}(M)$ cost per deleted node.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove the Affine Invariance Theorem: Compute $\nabla \bar{f}(y) = T^{-T}\nabla f(x)$ and $\nabla^2 \bar{f}(y) = T^{-T}\nabla^2 f(x) T^{-1}$. Invert the Hessian to show $\Delta y_{\text{nt}} = -(\nabla^2 \bar{f}(y))^{-1}\nabla \bar{f}(y) = T \Delta x_{\text{nt}}$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that the quadratic Taylor approximation error $f(x) - \inf_y \left( f(x) + \nabla f(x)^T(y-x) + \frac{1}{2}(y-x)^T \nabla^2 f(x)(y-x) \right)$ is exactly equal to $\frac{1}{2}\lambda(x)^2$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In an HNSW index with $N = 10^6$ vectors and degree $M = 32$, compare total computational operations for 100K deletions: (a) Immediate graph edge rewiring ($10^5 \times 32^2 \approx 1.02 \times 10^8$ operations), vs (b) Atomic bitset tombstones with a single batch vacuum pass ($3.2 \times 10^7$ operations, $>3.1\times$ computational reduction).
