### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Spectral Graph Theory* — Daniel A. Spielman / Fan Chung
* **Sections**: §1.1–1.3 (*The Graph Laplacian, Quadratic Forms & Connected Components*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Graph Laplacian Matrix**:
     * For undirected graph $G = (V, E)$ with adjacency $A$ and degree matrix $D = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, \dots, d_n)$:
       $$L = D - A$$
  2. **Laplacian Quadratic Form Identity**:
     $$\mathbf{x}^T L \mathbf{x} = \sum_{i=1}^n d_i x_i^2 - \sum_{i, j} A_{ij} x_i x_j = \sum_{(u, v) \in E} (x_u - x_v)^2$$
     * **Positive Semidefiniteness**: Since $(x_u - x_v)^2 \ge 0$, $\mathbf{x}^T L \mathbf{x} \ge 0$ for all $\mathbf{x} \implies L \succeq 0$.
  3. **Zero Eigenvalue & Connected Components**:
     * $L \mathbf{1} = (D - A)\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{d} - \mathbf{d} = \mathbf{0} \implies \lambda_1 = 0$ with constant eigenvector $\mathbf{v}_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbf{1}$.
     * **Theorem**: The multiplicity of $0$ as an eigenvalue of $L$ is equal to the exact number of connected components in graph $G$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the quadratic identity $\mathbf{x}^T L \mathbf{x} = \sum_{(u, v) \in E} (x_u - x_v)^2$ by expanding $\sum_{(u, v) \in E} (x_u^2 - 2 x_u x_v + x_v^2)$ and grouping terms by vertex degrees.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that if graph $G$ has $k$ connected components $V_1, \dots, V_k$, the indicator vectors $\mathbf{1}_{V_1}, \dots, \mathbf{1}_{V_k}$ are mutually orthogonal null-space vectors of $L$, proving $\operatorname{dim}(\operatorname{null}(L)) = k$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: On a 4-node ring graph $C_4$ with edges $\{(1, 2), (2, 3), (3, 4), (4, 1)\}$, write the explicit Laplacian matrix $L$ and compute all 4 eigenvalues: $\lambda \in \{0, 2, 2, 4\}$ with corresponding orthogonal eigenvectors.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Spectral Graph Theory* — Daniel A. Spielman / Fan Chung
* **Sections**: §1.4–1.6 (*The Normalized Graph Laplacian, Eigenvalue Bounds & Bipartiteness*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Normalized Graph Laplacian**:
     $$\mathcal{L} = D^{-1/2} L D^{-1/2} = I - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}$$
     * Quadratic Form:
       $$\mathbf{x}^T \mathcal{L} \mathbf{x} = \sum_{(u, v) \in E} \left( \frac{x_u}{\sqrt{d_u}} - \frac{x_v}{\sqrt{d_v}} \right)^2$$
  2. **Universal Eigenvalue Bounds**:
     $$0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_n \le 2$$
     * Trivial lower eigenpair: $\lambda_1 = 0$ with eigenvector $\mathbf{v}_1 = D^{1/2}\mathbf{1}$.
  3. **Bipartite Spectrum Theorem**:
     * **Theorem**: $\lambda_n = 2 \iff G$ contains a non-trivial bipartite connected component.
     * For bipartite graphs, the spectrum is symmetric around $1$: $\lambda_k = 2 - \lambda_{n-k+1}$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the quadratic form identity $\mathbf{x}^T \mathcal{L} \mathbf{x} = \sum_{(u, v) \in E} \left( \frac{x_u}{\sqrt{d_u}} - \frac{x_v}{\sqrt{d_v}} \right)^2$ by setting $\mathbf{y} = D^{-1/2}\mathbf{x}$ in the standard Laplacian quadratic form $\mathbf{y}^T L \mathbf{y}$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove the upper bound $\lambda_n \le 2$: use $(a - b)^2 \le 2(a^2 + b^2)$ to show $\mathbf{x}^T \mathcal{L} \mathbf{x} \le 2 \sum_{(u, v) \in E} \left( \frac{x_u^2}{d_u} + \frac{x_v^2}{d_v} \right) = 2 \sum_{u \in V} x_u^2 = 2 \|\mathbf{x}\|_2^2$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: On the complete bipartite graph $K_{m, n}$, write down the $A$ and $D$ matrices and derive the spectrum of $\mathcal{L}$: show that $\lambda_1 = 0$, $\lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_{m+n-1} = 1$, and $\lambda_{m+n} = 2$.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Spectral Graph Theory* — Daniel A. Spielman / Fan Chung
* **Sections**: §2.1–2.3 (*Graph Cuts, Conductance & The Cheeger Constant*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Graph Boundary & Volume**:
     * Boundary: $\partial S = E(S, \bar{S}) = \{(u, v) \in E \mid u \in S, v \notin S\}$.
     * Volume: $\operatorname{vol}(S) = \sum_{u \in S} d_u$.
  2. **Conductance (Cheeger Constant)**:
     $$\phi(G) = \min_{\emptyset \subset S \subset V} \frac{|E(S, \bar{S})|}{\min(\operatorname{vol}(S), \operatorname{vol}(\bar{S}))}$$
  3. **Cheeger's Inequality (Lower Bound)**:
     $$\frac{\lambda_2}{2} \le \phi(G) \le \sqrt{2 \lambda_2}$$
     * **Significance**: $\lambda_2$ (algebraic connectivity) strictly bounds the worst-case bottleneck cut in the graph. High $\lambda_2$ ensures fast greedy routing in HNSW without getting trapped in local clusters.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the lower Cheeger bound $\frac{\lambda_2}{2} \le \phi(G)$: For optimal cut $S$ with $\operatorname{vol}(S) \le \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{vol}(V)$, construct the test vector $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{1}_S - \frac{\operatorname{vol}(S)}{\operatorname{vol}(V)}\mathbf{1}$, verify $\mathbf{x} \perp D \mathbf{1}$, and show $\lambda_2 \le \frac{\mathbf{x}^T L \mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^T D \mathbf{x}} = \frac{|E(S, \bar{S})|}{\operatorname{vol}(S)(1 - \operatorname{vol}(S)/\operatorname{vol}(V))} \le 2 \phi(G)$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that complete graph $K_n$ has conductance $\phi(K_n) = \frac{n}{2(n-1)} \approx 0.5$, whereas a 1D path graph $P_n$ has $\phi(P_n) = \mathcal{O}(1/n)$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: On a barbell graph consisting of two cliques $K_n$ connected by a single bridge edge, calculate the exact conductance $\phi(G) \approx \frac{1}{n^2}$ and show that algebraic connectivity $\lambda_2 \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.
