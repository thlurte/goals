### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Trigonometry* — I.M. Gelfand & Mark Saul
* **Chapter**: Chapter 4 (*Addition Formulas*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Geometric Proof of the Cosine Subtraction Formula**:
     * Points $P = (\cos\alpha, \sin\alpha)$ and $Q = (\cos\beta, \sin\beta)$ on unit circle $S^1$.
     * Euclidean chord distance squared:
       $$d^2(P, Q) = (\cos\alpha - \cos\beta)^2 + (\sin\alpha - \sin\beta)^2 = 2 - 2(\cos\alpha\cos\beta + \sin\alpha\sin\beta)$$
     * Rotating the chord so $Q' = (1, 0)$ and $P' = (\cos(\alpha-\beta), \sin(\alpha-\beta))$ gives $d^2(P', Q') = 2 - 2\cos(\alpha-\beta)$.
     * Equating both distances establishes the fundamental identity:
       $$\cos(\alpha - \beta) = \cos\alpha \cos\beta + \sin\alpha \sin\beta$$
  2. **The Complete Addition Family**:
     $$\cos(\alpha + \beta) = \cos\alpha \cos\beta - \sin\alpha \sin\beta$$
     $$\sin(\alpha \pm \beta) = \sin\alpha \cos\beta \pm \cos\alpha \sin\beta$$
     $$\tan(\alpha \pm \beta) = \frac{\tan\alpha \pm \tan\beta}{1 \mp \tan\alpha \tan\beta}$$

* **Chapter**: Chapter 4 (*Double-Angle and Half-Angle Formulas*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Double-Angle Identities**:
     $$\cos(2\theta) = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$$
     $$\sin(2\theta) = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$$
     $$\tan(2\theta) = \frac{2\tan\theta}{1 - \tan^2\theta}$$
  2. **Half-Angle Power-Reduction Formulas**:
     $$\sin^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = \frac{1 - \cos\theta}{2}, \quad \cos^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = \frac{1 + \cos\theta}{2}$$

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Section**: §2.6 (*The Chain Rule*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Chain Rule for Composite Functions**:
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[f(g(x))\right] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) \quad \iff \quad \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}$$
  2. **Rigorous $\epsilon$-Limit Formulation (Handling $\Delta u = 0$)**:
     * Define the error function $\epsilon(k) = \frac{f(u+k) - f(u)}{k} - f'(u)$ for $k \ne 0$, and $\epsilon(0) = 0$.
     * Since $f$ is differentiable at $u$, $\lim_{k \to 0}\epsilon(k) = 0$.
     * Therefore, $\Delta y = \left[f'(u) + \epsilon(\Delta u)\right] \Delta u$.
     * Dividing by $\Delta x \ne 0$ and taking the limit $\Delta x \to 0$ (which forces $\Delta u \to 0$ by continuity of $g$):
       $$\frac{dy}{dx} = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \left[f'(u) + \epsilon(\Delta u)\right] \frac{\Delta u}{\Delta x} = f'(u) \cdot g'(x)$$
  3. **Generalized Power & Trigonometric Rules**:
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[u(x)\right]^n = n [u(x)]^{n-1} u'(x), \quad \frac{d}{dx}\cos(u(x)) = -\sin(u(x)) u'(x)$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:

  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Differentiate $y = \cos^3\left(\sqrt{x^2 + 1}\right)$ step-by-step showing every intermediate composite layer.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Use implicit differentiation on $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ to find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ at $(3, 4)$.
