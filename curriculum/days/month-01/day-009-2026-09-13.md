### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §3.1–3.2 (*The Exponential $e^x$, The Natural Logarithm $\ln x$, and Logarithmic Differentiation*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Unique Derivative Property of $e^x$**:
     * Euler's number $e$ is defined as the unique real number such that:
       $$\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{e^h - 1}{h} = 1 \implies \frac{d}{dx}e^x = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{e^{x+h} - e^x}{h} = e^x \lim_{h \to 0}\frac{e^h - 1}{h} = e^x$$
  2. **Derivative of the Natural Logarithm**:
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\ln x = \frac{1}{x} \quad (x > 0)$$
  3. **Logarithmic Differentiation**:
     * For complex products and powers $y = f(x)^{g(x)}$, taking natural logs simplifies the differentiation:
       $$\ln y = g(x) \ln f(x) \implies \frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} = g'(x)\ln f(x) + g(x)\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove $\frac{d}{dx}\ln x = \frac{1}{x}$ directly from the limit definition using the limit $\lim_{u \to 0}(1 + u)^{1/u} = e$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Using logarithmic differentiation, prove that $\frac{d}{dx}(x^x) = x^x(1 + \ln x)$ for $x > 0$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Compute the derivative of $f(x) = \frac{e^{4x} \sqrt{x^2 + 9}}{(2x + 1)^3}$ using logarithmic differentiation.

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Section**: §2.7 (*Inverse Functions and Their Derivatives*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Inverse Function Derivative Theorem**:
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[f^{-1}(x)\right] = \frac{1}{f'\left(f^{-1}(x)\right)} \quad \text{provided } f'\left(f^{-1}(x)\right) \ne 0$$
  2. **Derivatives of Inverse Trigonometric Functions**:
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\arcsin x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} \quad (|x| < 1)$$
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\arccos x = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} \quad (|x| < 1)$$
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\arctan x = \frac{1}{1 + x^2} \quad (x \in \mathbb{R})$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive $\frac{d}{dx}\arcsin x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$ using implicit differentiation on $x = \sin y$ for $y \in (-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2})$, showing why $\cos y = +\sqrt{1 - \sin^2 y} = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive $\frac{d}{dx}\arctan x = \frac{1}{1 + x^2}$ using implicit differentiation on $x = \tan y$ and the identity $\sec^2 y = 1 + \tan^2 y = 1 + x^2$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Find the derivative of $f(x) = \arctan\left(\frac{2x}{1 - x^2}\right)$ for $|x| < 1$ and simplify the result to $\frac{2}{1 + x^2}$.
