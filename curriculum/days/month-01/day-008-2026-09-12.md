### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Trigonometry* — I.M. Gelfand & Mark Saul
* **Chapters**: Chapter 4 (*Angles and Rotations*) & Chapter 5 (*Radian Measure*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Angles in Standard Position & The Unit Circle**:
     * Ray rotation from the positive $x$-axis. Coordinates on the unit circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ define trigonometric functions for any real angle: $\cos\theta = x$ and $\sin\theta = y$.
  2. **Parity and Reflection Symmetries**:
     $$\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta \quad (\text{Even Symmetry}), \quad \sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta \quad (\text{Odd Symmetry})$$
     $$\cos(\pi - \theta) = -\cos\theta, \quad \sin(\pi - \theta) = \sin\theta \quad (\text{Supplementary Reflection across $y$-axis})$$
  3. **Radian Measure & The Wrapping Function**:
     * Definition of radian angle $\theta = \frac{s}{r}$ (where $s$ is arc length). Sector area $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.
     * The wrapping function $W: \mathbb{R} \to \mathcal{S}^1$ wrapping the real number line continuously onto the unit circle, yielding fundamental periodicity: $\cos(\theta + 2k\pi) = \cos\theta$, $\sin(\theta + 2k\pi) = \sin\theta$ ($\forall k \in \mathbb{Z}$).

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Trigonometry* — I.M. Gelfand & Mark Saul
* **Chapter**: Chapter 6 (*The Addition Formulas*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Geometric Proof of the Cosine Subtraction Formula**:
     * Points $P = (\cos\alpha, \sin\alpha)$ and $Q = (\cos\beta, \sin\beta)$ on unit circle $S^1$.
     * Euclidean chord distance squared:
       $$d^2(P, Q) = (\cos\alpha - \cos\beta)^2 + (\sin\alpha - \sin\beta)^2 = 2 - 2(\cos\alpha\cos\beta + \sin\alpha\sin\beta)$$
     * Rotating the chord so $Q' = (1, 0)$ and $P' = (\cos(\alpha-\beta), \sin(\alpha-\beta))$ gives $d^2(P', Q') = 2 - 2\cos(\alpha-\beta)$.
     * Equating both distances establishes the fundamental identity:
       $$\cos(\alpha - \beta) = \cos\alpha \cos\beta + \sin\alpha \sin\beta$$
  2. **The Complete Addition Family & Double/Half-Angle Formulas**:
     $$\cos(\alpha + \beta) = \cos\alpha \cos\beta - \sin\alpha \sin\beta$$
     $$\sin(\alpha \pm \beta) = \sin\alpha \cos\beta \pm \cos\alpha \sin\beta$$
     $$\cos(2\theta) = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta, \quad \sin(2\theta) = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$$
     $$\sin^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = \frac{1 - \cos\theta}{2}, \quad \cos^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = \frac{1 + \cos\theta}{2}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Complete the step-by-step Cartesian chord proof of $\cos(\alpha - \beta) = \cos\alpha\cos\beta + \sin\alpha\sin\beta$ in your notebook.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Using the addition formula with $\alpha = \beta = \theta$, derive $\cos(2\theta) = 2\cos^2\theta - 1$ and the half-angle formula $\tan(\frac{\theta}{2}) = \frac{1 - \cos\theta}{\sin\theta}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Compute exact radical values for $\sin(15^\circ) = \sin(\frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{\pi}{6})$ and $\cos(\frac{\pi}{8})$ without decimals.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §2.4–2.6 (*The Fundamental Trig Limit, Derivatives of Sine & Cosine, and The Chain Rule*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Geometric Squeeze Theorem for Radian Angles**:
     * With radian measure established from Gelfand Chapter 5 ($s = r\theta$), consider a sector on the unit circle $r=1$:
       $$\frac{1}{2}\sin\theta \cos\theta < \frac{1}{2}\theta < \frac{1}{2}\tan\theta \implies \cos\theta < \frac{\sin\theta}{\theta} < \frac{1}{\cos\theta}$$
     * As $\theta \to 0$, $\cos\theta \to 1$, squeezing the ratio: $\lim_{\theta \to 0}\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta} = 1$.
  2. **Derivative of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ from First Principles**:
     * Applying the Gelfand Chapter 6 addition formula $\sin(x+h) = \sin x \cos h + \cos x \sin h$:
       $$\frac{d}{dx}\sin x = \lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\sin(x+h) - \sin x}{h} = \sin x \lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\cos h - 1}{h} + \cos x \lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\sin h}{h} = \cos x$$
  3. **The Chain Rule for Composite Functions**:
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[f(g(x))\right] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) \quad \iff \quad \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Complete the geometric sector squeeze proof showing $\lim_{\theta \to 0}\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta} = 1$ and $\lim_{\theta \to 0}\frac{\cos\theta - 1}{\theta} = 0$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive $\frac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x$ from first principles using the addition formula from Part 2.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Differentiate $y = \cos^3\left(\sqrt{x^2 + 1}\right)$ step-by-step applying the Chain Rule across all three composite layers.
