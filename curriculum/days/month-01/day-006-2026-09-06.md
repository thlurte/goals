### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Trigonometry* — I.M. Gelfand & Mark Saul
* **Chapter**: Chapter 3 (*Radians and Periodic Functions*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Periodicity & Fundamental Periods**:
     $$\sin(\theta + 2k\pi) = \sin\theta, \quad \cos(\theta + 2k\pi) = \cos\theta \quad (\forall k \in \mathbb{Z})$$
     $$\tan(\theta + k\pi) = \tan\theta \quad (\text{Period } T = \pi)$$
  2. **Parity (Symmetry)**:
     $$\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta \quad (\text{Even Symmetry}), \quad \sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta \quad (\text{Odd Symmetry})$$
  3. **Phase Shifts & Co-Function Identities**:
     $$\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right) = \cos\theta, \quad \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right) = \sin\theta$$
  4. **Sinusoidal Parameterization**: $f(t) = A \sin(\omega t + \phi)$, where amplitude is $|A|$, angular frequency is $\omega$, period is $T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$, and phase shift is $-\frac{\phi}{\omega}$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Using the geometric symmetry of the unit circle, prove that $\cos(\pi - \theta) = -\cos\theta$ and $\sin(\pi - \theta) = \sin\theta$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Sketch $y = 3\cos(2x - \frac{\pi}{3})$ over the domain $[0, 2\pi]$. Compute the exact coordinates of all local maxima, local minima, and zero-crossings.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Simplify the expression analytically:
    $$\frac{\sin(-\theta)}{\cos(-\theta)} + \frac{\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right)}$$

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §1.1–1.5 (*Introduction to Calculus: Limits, Continuity, and the Intermediate Value Theorem*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Formal $\epsilon$-$\delta$ Definition of a Limit**:
     $$\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L \iff \forall \epsilon > 0, \exists \delta > 0 \text{ such that } 0 < |x - c| < \delta \implies |f(x) - L| < \epsilon$$
  2. **One-Sided Limits & Existence**: $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$ if and only if $\lim_{x \to c^+} f(x) = \lim_{x \to c^-} f(x) = L$.
  3. **Definition of Continuity**: A function $f$ is continuous at $x=c$ iff: (1) $f(c)$ is defined, (2) $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ exists, and (3) $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = f(c)$.
  4. **Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT)**: If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $u$ is any value between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$, then there exists at least one $c \in [a, b]$ such that $f(c) = u$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Using the formal $\epsilon$-$\delta$ definition, prove that $\lim_{x \to 3} (2x + 5) = 11$. Explicitly show the construction of $\delta(\epsilon) = \frac{\epsilon}{2}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Evaluate the limit algebraically without L'Hôpital's Rule:
    $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 9} - 3}{x^2}$$
  - [ ] **Proof 3**: Using the geometric squeeze theorem on a unit circle sector ($\cos\theta \le \frac{\sin\theta}{\theta} \le 1$ for $\theta \in (-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2})$), prove:
    $$\lim_{\theta \to 0} \frac{\sin\theta}{\theta} = 1$$
