### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §8.4–8.6 (*Taylor Polynomials, Taylor Series & Lagrange Remainder Theorem*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Taylor Series Centered at $x = a$**:
     $$f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!} (x - a)^n$$
  2. **Taylor's Theorem with Lagrange Remainder Form**:
     $$f(x) = P_n(x) + R_n(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n \frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x - a)^k + \frac{f^{(n+1)}(c)}{(n+1)!}(x - a)^{n+1} \quad (c \in [a, x])$$
  3. **Canonical Maclaurin Expansions**:
     $$e^x = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{x^n}{n!}, \quad \cos x = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{(2n)!}, \quad \ln(1 + x) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{n-1} x^n}{n} \quad (|x| < 1)$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the Maclaurin series for $e^x$ and prove that the Lagrange remainder $\lim_{n \to \infty} R_n(x) = 0$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$, establishing uniform convergence.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Find the 4th-degree Taylor polynomial $P_4(x)$ for $\cos x$ centered at $a=0$ and compute a strict upper bound for $|R_4(x)|$ on $[-\frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{\pi}{4}]$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Approximate the definite integral $\int_0^1 e^{-x^2} dx$ using the first 3 terms of the Taylor series and bound the approximation error.

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §1.1–1.2 (*Vectors, Linear Combinations, Dot Products & The Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Inner Product & Euclidean Metric in $\mathbb{R}^d$**:
     $$\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = \mathbf{u}^T \mathbf{v} = \sum_{i=1}^d u_i v_i, \quad \|\mathbf{u}\|_2 = \sqrt{\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{u} \rangle}$$
  2. **The Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality**:
     $$|\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle| \le \|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2 \quad \text{with equality if and only if } \mathbf{u} = c \mathbf{v}$$
  3. **The Triangle Inequality in $\mathbb{R}^d$**:
     $$\|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}\|_2 \le \|\mathbf{u}\|_2 + \|\mathbf{v}\|_2$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove Cauchy-Schwarz rigorously using the quadratic polynomial $P(t) = \|\mathbf{u} + t \mathbf{v}\|_2^2 = \|\mathbf{u}\|_2^2 + 2t\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle + t^2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2^2 \ge 0$, whose discriminant $\Delta = 4\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle^2 - 4\|\mathbf{u}\|_2^2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2^2 \le 0$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Use Cauchy-Schwarz to prove the Triangle Inequality by expanding $\|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}\|_2^2$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For $\mathbf{u} = (1, 2, 2, 4)$ and $\mathbf{v} = (2, -1, 0, 2)$, compute $\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle$, $\|\mathbf{u}\|$, $\|\mathbf{v}\|$, and the exact cosine of their angle.
