### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Topic**: *Complex Numbers, Polar Coordinates & De Moivre's Theorem* (Bridging Trigonometry to Spectral Analysis)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Cartesian vs. Polar Representations**:
     $$z = x + iy = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta) = r e^{i\theta} \quad \text{where } r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}, \, \theta = \operatorname{atan2}(y, x)$$
  2. **Geometric Multiplication (Scaling and Rotation)**:
     $$z_1 z_2 = r_1 r_2 \left[\cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2) + i\sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2)\right]$$
  3. **De Moivre's Theorem & Roots of Unity**:
     $$(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^n = \cos(n\theta) + i\sin(n\theta), \quad z^n = 1 \implies z_k = \cos\left(\frac{2\pi k}{n}\right) + i\sin\left(\frac{2\pi k}{n}\right)$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove De Moivre's Theorem for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ by induction using the angle addition formulas $\cos(\alpha+\beta)$ and $\sin(\alpha+\beta)$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Find all 4 complex roots of $z^4 = -16$ in both polar $r e^{i\theta}$ and Cartesian $a+bi$ forms.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Use De Moivre's theorem with $n=3$ to express $\cos(3\theta)$ and $\sin(3\theta)$ strictly in terms of powers of $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Topic**: *Euler's Formula, Complex Exponentials & Hyperspherical Geometry*
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Euler's Fundamental Formula**:
     $$e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$$
  2. **Taylor Series Derivation**:
     $$e^{i\theta} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(i\theta)^n}{n!} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^k \theta^{2k}}{(2k)!} + i \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^k \theta^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$$
  3. **Spherical Voronoi Geometry on Hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$**:
     * For unit-normalized vectors ($\|x\|_2 = \|c_k\|_2 = 1$), Euclidean distance minimization is mathematically equivalent to cosine / inner product maximization:
       $$\arg\min_k \|x - c_k\|_2^2 = \arg\min_k (2 - 2\langle x, c_k \rangle) = \arg\max_k \langle x, c_k \rangle$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Write out the full power-series proof of Euler's formula $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$ separating even (real) and odd (imaginary) terms.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that the sum of all $n$-th roots of unity vanishes: $\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} e^{i \frac{2\pi k}{n}} = 0$ for all $n \ge 2$ using the finite geometric series formula.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Evaluate $\left(\frac{1 + i\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{12}$ using Euler's formula.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §8.1–8.3 (*Infinite Sequences, Series & Classical Convergence Tests*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Series Convergence & Divergence Test**:
     $$\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n = \lim_{k \to \infty} s_k \quad \text{where } s_k = \sum_{n=1}^k a_n; \quad \lim_{n \to \infty} a_n \ne 0 \implies \sum a_n \text{ diverges}$$
  2. **The Geometric Series Formula**:
     $$\sum_{n=0}^\infty a r^n = \frac{a}{1 - r} \quad \iff \quad |r| < 1$$
  3. **The Integral Test & $p$-Series Theorem**:
     $$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^p} \quad \text{converges if and only if } p > 1$$
  4. **The Ratio Test**:
     $$L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left| \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} \right| \implies \begin{cases} L < 1 & \text{converges absolutely} \\ L > 1 & \text{diverges} \\ L = 1 & \text{inconclusive} \end{cases}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove the finite partial sum formula $s_n = \frac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r}$ and take the limit as $n \to \infty$ for $|r| < 1$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Use the improper integral $\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^p} dx$ to prove the $p$-series convergence criterion.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Test the convergence of $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{n^4 3^n}{n!}$ using the Ratio Test.
