### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §5.4–5.5 (*Integration by Parts and Reduction Formulas*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Integration by Parts Formulation**:
     $$\int u(x) v'(x) dx = u(x)v(x) - \int v(x) u'(x) dx \quad \iff \quad \int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du$$
  2. **Definite Integration by Parts**:
     $$\int_a^b u \, dv = \left[ u(x)v(x) \right]_a^b - \int_a^b v \, du$$
  3. **The LIATE Selection Heuristic**: Priority for choosing $u$: **L**ogarithmic, **I**nverse Trigonometric, **A**lgebraic, **T**rigonometric, **E**xponential.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the Integration by Parts formula directly from the differential Product Rule $d(uv) = u \, dv + v \, du$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Evaluate $\int x^2 e^{2x} dx$ using the tabular integration-by-parts method.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Evaluate $\int \arcsin x \, dx$ by setting $u = \arcsin x, dv = dx$ and evaluating the resulting algebraic integral.

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §5.5–5.6 (*Trigonometric Integrals & Trigonometric Substitutions*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Integrals of Trigonometric Products $\int \sin^m x \cos^n x dx$**:
     * If $n$ is odd ($n = 2k + 1$): Split off $\cos x dx$, convert remaining $\cos^{2k} x = (1 - \sin^2 x)^k$, and substitute $u = \sin x$.
     * If $m$ is odd ($m = 2k + 1$): Split off $\sin x dx$, convert $\sin^{2k} x = (1 - \cos^2 x)^k$, and substitute $u = \cos x$.
     * If both are even: Apply half-angle power reduction identities repeatedly:
       $$\sin^2 x = \frac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}, \quad \cos^2 x = \frac{1 + \cos 2x}{2}$$
  2. **Trigonometric Substitution for Radical Forms**:
     * For $\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}$: Substitute $x = a \sin\theta, dx = a \cos\theta d\theta \implies \sqrt{a^2 - x^2} = a \cos\theta$.
     * For $\sqrt{a^2 + x^2}$: Substitute $x = a \tan\theta, dx = a \sec^2\theta d\theta \implies \sqrt{a^2 + x^2} = a \sec\theta$.
     * For $\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}$: Substitute $x = a \sec\theta, dx = a \sec\theta \tan\theta d\theta \implies \sqrt{x^2 - a^2} = a \tan\theta$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Problem 1**: Evaluate $\int \sin^3 x \cos^4 x dx$ using $u = \cos x$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Evaluate $\int \frac{1}{(x^2 + 4)^{3/2}} dx$ using the trigonometric substitution $x = 2 \tan\theta$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Evaluate $\int \sqrt{9 - x^2} dx$ using $x = 3 \sin\theta$ and verify that the definite integral from $0$ to $3$ gives the area of a quarter circle $\frac{9\pi}{4}$.
