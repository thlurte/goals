### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §2.1–2.3 (*The Derivative of a Function, Powers and Polynomials, The Slope and the Tangent Line*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Derivative from First Principles**:
     $$f'(x) = \frac{df}{dx} = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{f(x + \Delta x) - f(x)}{\Delta x} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$$
  2. **Linearity of Differentiation**:
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[\alpha f(x) + \beta g(x)\right] = \alpha f'(x) + \beta g'(x) \quad (\forall \alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R})$$
  3. **The General Power Rule Proof for Integer Powers**: For any $n \in \mathbb{N}$, using the binomial theorem $(x+h)^n = x^n + n x^{n-1}h + \binom{n}{2}x^{n-2}h^2 + \dots$:
     $$\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(x^n + n x^{n-1}h + \mathcal{O}(h^2)) - x^n}{h} = n x^{n-1}$$
  4. **The Best Linear Approximation**: The tangent line at $(x_0, f(x_0))$ is $L(x) = f(x_0) + f'(x_0)(x - x_0)$, where error satisfies $\lim_{x \to x_0} \frac{|f(x) - L(x)|}{|x - x_0|} = 0$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove that $\frac{d}{dx}(x^3) = 3x^2$ directly using the limit of the difference quotient.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that $\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = -\frac{1}{x^2}$ using the definition of the derivative for $x \ne 0$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Find the exact equation of the tangent line to $f(x) = x^4 - 2x^2 + 3$ at $x = -1$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §2.4–2.5 (*The Product Rule, Quotient Rule, and Trigonometric Derivatives*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Product Rule Derivation**:
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[u(x)v(x)\right] = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{u(x+h)v(x+h) - u(x)v(x)}{h} = u'(x)v(x) + u(x)v'(x)$$
  2. **The Quotient Rule**:
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}\right] = \frac{u'(x)v(x) - u(x)v'(x)}{\left[v(x)\right]^2} \quad (v(x) \ne 0)$$
  3. **Derivatives of Trigonometric Functions**:
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x, \quad \frac{d}{dx}\cos x = -\sin x$$
     $$\frac{d}{dx}\tan x = \sec^2 x, \quad \frac{d}{dx}\sec x = \sec x \tan x$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove that $\frac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x$ from the limit definition using the angle addition identity $\sin(x+h) = \sin x \cos h + \cos x \sin h$ and the fundamental limits $\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\sin h}{h} = 1$ and $\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\cos h - 1}{h} = 0$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the Quotient Rule directly by expressing $\frac{u}{v} = u \cdot v^{-1}$ and applying the Product Rule and Power Rule.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Find the derivative of $f(x) = \frac{x^2 \sin x}{\cos x + 1}$ and compute $f'(0)$.
