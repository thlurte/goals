### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §5.5 (*Real Symmetric Matrices & The Spectral Theorem $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{Q}^T$*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Real Eigenvalues of Symmetric Matrices**:
     * Let $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{A}^T \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$. Then every eigenvalue $\lambda$ is strictly real:
       $$\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \lambda \mathbf{x} \implies \bar{\mathbf{x}}^T \mathbf{A} \mathbf{x} = \lambda \bar{\mathbf{x}}^T \mathbf{x}$$
     * Taking complex conjugate transpose: $\bar{\mathbf{x}}^T \mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{x} = \bar{\lambda} \bar{\mathbf{x}}^T \mathbf{x}$. Since $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{A}^T$:
       $$\lambda \|\mathbf{x}\|^2 = \bar{\lambda} \|\mathbf{x}\|^2 \implies \lambda = \bar{\lambda} \implies \lambda \in \mathbb{R}$$
  2. **Orthogonality of Distinct Eigenvectors**:
     * If $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_1 = \lambda_1 \mathbf{x}_1$ and $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_2 = \lambda_2 \mathbf{x}_2$ with $\lambda_1 \ne \lambda_2$:
       $$\lambda_1 (\mathbf{x}_1^T \mathbf{x}_2) = (\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_1)^T \mathbf{x}_2 = \mathbf{x}_1^T \mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{x}_2 = \mathbf{x}_1^T \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_2 = \lambda_2 (\mathbf{x}_1^T \mathbf{x}_2) \implies (\lambda_1 - \lambda_2)(\mathbf{x}_1^T \mathbf{x}_2) = 0 \implies \mathbf{x}_1 \perp \mathbf{x}_2$$
  3. **The Spectral Theorem (Principal Axis Theorem)**:
     $$\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{\Lambda} \mathbf{Q}^T = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i \mathbf{q}_i \mathbf{q}_i^T$$
     * Every real symmetric matrix has a full set of $n$ orthonormal eigenvectors that diagonalize $\mathbf{A}$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove step-by-step that all eigenvalues of a real symmetric matrix are real using the complex Hermitian inner product identity $\bar{\mathbf{x}}^T \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{A}\bar{\mathbf{x}}$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that eigenvectors $\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2$ corresponding to distinct eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \ne \lambda_2$ of $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{A}^T$ are orthogonal.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For symmetric matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$, compute eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$, find normalized orthonormal eigenvectors $\mathbf{q}_1, \mathbf{q}_2$, and verify $\mathbf{A} = \lambda_1 \mathbf{q}_1 \mathbf{q}_1^T + \lambda_2 \mathbf{q}_2 \mathbf{q}_2^T$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §5.6 (*Positive Definite Matrices, Sylvester's Criterion & Cholesky Factorization*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The 5 Equivalent Characterizations of Positive Definiteness**:
     * **Energy Test**: $\mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{A} \mathbf{x} > 0$ for all non-zero vectors $\mathbf{x} \ne \mathbf{0}$.
     * **Eigenvalue Test**: All eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{A}^T$ are strictly positive: $\lambda_i > 0$.
     * **Sylvester's Determinant Test**: All $n$ leading principal minors are positive: $\det(\mathbf{A}_1) > 0, \det(\mathbf{A}_2) > 0, \dots, \det(\mathbf{A}_n) > 0$.
     * **Pivot Test**: All $n$ pivots in Gaussian elimination (without row exchanges) are strictly positive: $d_i > 0$.
     * **Matrix Factorization**: $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{R}^T \mathbf{R}$ with independent columns in $\mathbf{R}$.
  2. **The Cholesky Factorization $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^T$**:
     * Since $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{L}^T$ and all pivots $d_i > 0$, define $\mathbf{D}^{1/2} = \operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{d_1}, \dots, \sqrt{d_n})$:
       $$\mathbf{A} = (\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D}^{1/2})(\mathbf{D}^{1/2}\mathbf{L}^T) = \mathbf{L}_{\text{chol}} \mathbf{L}_{\text{chol}}^T$$
     * $\mathbf{L}_{\text{chol}}$ is lower triangular with positive diagonal elements $\ell_{ii} = \sqrt{d_i} > 0$, computed in $\frac{1}{3}n^3$ flops (twice as fast as standard $LU$!).
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove using the Spectral Theorem $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{Q}^T$ that $\mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} > 0 \iff \lambda_i > 0$ for all $i$, by letting $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{Q}^T \mathbf{x}$ and expanding $\mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \sum \lambda_i y_i^2$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Test matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ for positive definiteness using: (a) Sylvester's principal minors, (b) Gaussian elimination pivots, and (c) compute the exact Cholesky factor $\mathbf{L}_{\text{chol}}$.
  - [ ] **Proof 3**: If $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are symmetric positive definite, prove that $\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{B}$ is strictly positive definite, but give a $2 \times 2$ counterexample showing $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}$ is not necessarily symmetric or positive definite.
