### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §4.1–4.4 (*The 3 Axioms of Determinants, Permutations, Cofactors & Inverse Formula*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The 3 Axiomatic Properties of Determinants**:
     * **Axiom 1**: $\det(\mathbf{I}) = 1$.
     * **Axiom 2**: Interchanging two rows multiplies the determinant by $-1$.
     * **Axiom 3**: Linearity in every row individually: $\det\begin{pmatrix} t\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{a}' \\ \mathbf{b} \end{pmatrix} = t \det\begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{a} \\ \mathbf{b} \end{pmatrix} + \det\begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{a}' \\ \mathbf{b} \end{pmatrix}$.
  2. **Core Multiplicative & Invertibility Identities**:
     * $\det(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}) = \det(\mathbf{A})\det(\mathbf{B})$.
     * $\det(\mathbf{A}^T) = \det(\mathbf{A})$.
     * $\det(\mathbf{A}) \ne 0 \iff \mathbf{A}$ is invertible ($\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A}) = n$).
  3. **The Cofactor Matrix & The Adjugate Inverse**:
     * The $(i, j)$ cofactor: $C_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j} \det(\mathbf{M}_{ij})$, where $\mathbf{M}_{ij}$ is the $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ submatrix deleting row $i$ and column $j$.
     * Exact inverse formula:
       $$\mathbf{A} \mathbf{C}^T = (\det \mathbf{A})\mathbf{I} \implies \mathbf{A}^{-1} = \frac{\mathbf{C}^T}{\det \mathbf{A}}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove using Axiom 2 and Axiom 3 that if two rows of $\mathbf{A}$ are identical, then $\det(\mathbf{A}) = 0$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove the fundamental cofactor identity $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}^T = (\det \mathbf{A})\mathbf{I}$ by showing that the off-diagonal entries represent the cofactor expansion of a matrix with two identical rows.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 4 & 1 & 8 \end{pmatrix}$, compute $\det(\mathbf{A})$ using pivot elimination, construct the $3 \times 3$ cofactor matrix $\mathbf{C}$, and calculate $\mathbf{A}^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det \mathbf{A}}\mathbf{C}^T$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §5.1–5.2 (*Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors, The Characteristic Equation & Matrix Diagonalization*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Eigenvalue Definition & Characteristic Polynomial**:
     $$\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \lambda \mathbf{x} \iff (\mathbf{A} - \lambda \mathbf{I})\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0} \implies p(\lambda) = \det(\mathbf{A} - \lambda \mathbf{I}) = 0$$
  2. **Trace & Determinant Invariants**:
     $$\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{A}) = \sum_{i=1}^n a_{ii}, \quad \prod_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \det(\mathbf{A})$$
  3. **Matrix Diagonalization**:
     * If $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ has $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors $\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_n$:
       $$\mathbf{A}\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{A}[\mathbf{x}_1 \dots \mathbf{x}_n] = [\lambda_1 \mathbf{x}_1 \dots \lambda_n \mathbf{x}_n] = \mathbf{S}\mathbf{\Lambda} \implies \mathbf{A} = \mathbf{S} \mathbf{\Lambda} \mathbf{S}^{-1}$$
  4. **Matrix Powers & Exponential Growth**:
     $$\mathbf{A}^k = (\mathbf{S}\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{S}^{-1})^k = \mathbf{S}\mathbf{\Lambda}^k \mathbf{S}^{-1} = \mathbf{S} \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1^k, \dots, \lambda_n^k) \mathbf{S}^{-1}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove that eigenvectors $\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_k$ corresponding to distinct eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \ne \lambda_2 \ne \dots \ne \lambda_k$ are linearly independent by induction.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that $\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{A}) = \sum \lambda_i$ and $\det(\mathbf{A}) = \prod \lambda_i$ by matching polynomial coefficients in $\det(\mathbf{A} - \lambda \mathbf{I}) = \prod_{i=1}^n (\lambda_i - \lambda)$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$, compute the eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$, find their eigenvectors, construct eigenvector matrix $\mathbf{S}$ and diagonal matrix $\mathbf{\Lambda}$, and compute $\mathbf{A}^{10}$ explicitly.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §5.3–5.4 (*Differential Equations, Matrix Exponential $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$ & System Stability*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Linear Systems of Differential Equations**:
     $$\frac{d\mathbf{u}}{dt} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}(t) \quad \text{with initial condition } \mathbf{u}(0)$$
  2. **The Matrix Exponential $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$**:
     $$e^{\mathbf{A}t} = \mathbf{I} + \mathbf{A}t + \frac{(\mathbf{A}t)^2}{2!} + \frac{(\mathbf{A}t)^3}{3!} + \dots = \mathbf{S} \operatorname{diag}\left(e^{\lambda_1 t}, \dots, e^{\lambda_n t}\right) \mathbf{S}^{-1}$$
     * The exact analytic solution: $\mathbf{u}(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t} \mathbf{u}(0) = \mathbf{S} e^{\mathbf{\Lambda} t} \mathbf{S}^{-1} \mathbf{u}(0) = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i e^{\lambda_i t} \mathbf{x}_i$.
  3. **Dynamical Stability Criterion**:
     * The origin $\mathbf{0}$ is asymptotically stable $\iff \operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i) < 0$ for all eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}$.
     * Pure imaginary eigenvalues $\lambda = \pm i\omega$ generate undamped periodic oscillations (harmonics).
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove $\frac{d}{dt} e^{\mathbf{A}t} = \mathbf{A} e^{\mathbf{A}t}$ by differentiating the power series definition $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{\mathbf{A}^k t^k}{k!}$ term-by-term.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Solve the ODE system $\frac{d\mathbf{u}}{dt} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}\mathbf{u}$ with initial state $\mathbf{u}(0) = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$. Find eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$, compute $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$, and determine the long-term behavior as $t \to \infty$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -9 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, compute the eigenvalues $\lambda = \pm 3i$, find $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$ using Euler's formula, and prove that solutions describe closed harmonic ellipses in phase space.
