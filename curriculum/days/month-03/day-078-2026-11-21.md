### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §6.3 (*The Singular Value Decomposition (SVD): $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^T$*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)**:
     * For any matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ of rank $r$:
       $$\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{V}^T = \sum_{i=1}^r \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{v}_i^T$$
     * $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}$ is orthogonal: columns $\mathbf{u}_i$ are eigenvectors of $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^T$.
     * $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is orthogonal: columns $\mathbf{v}_i$ are eigenvectors of $\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{A}$.
     * $\mathbf{\Sigma} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ is diagonal with singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_r > 0$ where $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i(\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{A})}$.
  2. **The Geometric Action of SVD**:
     * SVD maps an orthonormal basis of the row space into an orthogonal basis of the column space:
       $$\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}_i = \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i \quad (1 \le i \le r), \quad \mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}_i = \mathbf{0} \quad (r < i \le n)$$
     * Unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^n \xrightarrow{\mathbf{A}}$ hyper-ellipsoid in $\mathbb{R}^m$ with semi-axes $\sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove that $\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{A} \mathbf{v}_i = \sigma_i^2 \mathbf{v}_i$ and $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{u}_i = \sigma_i^2 \mathbf{u}_i$ by multiplying $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^T$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Compute the complete SVD $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^T$ by hand for rank-1 matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, identifying $\sigma_1$, $\mathbf{u}_1$, and $\mathbf{v}_1$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, compute its eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ and singular values $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$, explaining why $\sigma_i = |\lambda_i|$ for symmetric matrices.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §6.3 (*Truncated SVD & The Eckart–Young–Mirsky Low-Rank Approximation Theorem*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Rank-$k$ Truncated SVD**:
     $$\mathbf{A}_k = \sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{v}_i^T = \mathbf{U}_k \mathbf{\Sigma}_k \mathbf{V}_k^T \quad (k < r)$$
  2. **The Eckart–Young–Mirsky Theorem**:
     * Among all matrices $\mathbf{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ with $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{B}) \le k$, the truncated SVD matrix $\mathbf{A}_k$ is the unique minimizer of the approximation error:
       * **Spectral Norm Error**:
         $$\min_{\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{B}) \le k} \|\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\|_2 = \|\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{A}_k\|_2 = \sigma_{k+1}$$
       * **Frobenius Norm Error**:
         $$\min_{\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{B}) \le k} \|\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\|_F^2 = \|\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{A}_k\|_F^2 = \sum_{j=k+1}^r \sigma_j^2$$
  3. **Dimensionality Reduction in Search**:
     * Truncating a $10^6 \times 768$ embedding matrix to rank $k=256$ drops $66\%$ of memory while preserving the maximal possible variance.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove that $\|\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{A}_k\|_2 = \sigma_{k+1}$ by expanding the error matrix $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{A}_k = \sum_{i=k+1}^r \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{v}_i^T$ and using the definition of the induced $L_2$ matrix norm.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Complete the Eckart-Young proof: for any rank-$k$ matrix $\mathbf{B}$, show there exists a unit vector $\mathbf{x} \in \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_{k+1}\} \cap N(\mathbf{B})$, and prove that $\|\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\|_2^2 \ge \|(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B})\mathbf{x}\|^2 = \|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\|^2 \ge \sigma_{k+1}^2$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$, compute its SVD, find the optimal rank-1 matrix $\mathbf{A}_1$, and compute both the spectral error $\sigma_2$ and Frobenius error $\|\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{A}_1\|_F$.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §6.7 (*Principal Component Analysis (PCA) & Total Variance Maximization*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Sample Covariance Matrix**:
     * Given mean-centered data matrix $\mathbf{X}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$:
       $$\mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{N-1} \mathbf{X}_0^T \mathbf{X}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$$
     * $\mathbf{S}$ is symmetric positive semi-definite: $\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{\Lambda} \mathbf{Q}^T$ with $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_d \ge 0$.
  2. **Variance Maximization & Rayleigh Quotient**:
     * The first principal direction $\mathbf{q}_1$ maximizes projected variance:
       $$\max_{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 = 1} \operatorname{Var}(\mathbf{X}_0 \mathbf{u}) = \max_{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 = 1} \mathbf{u}^T \mathbf{S} \mathbf{u} = \lambda_1$$
     * Subsequent directions $\mathbf{q}_k$ maximize variance orthogonal to $\mathbf{q}_1, \dots, \mathbf{q}_{k-1}$.
  3. **Total Variance Conservation**:
     $$\text{Total Variance} = \sum_{i=1}^d \operatorname{Var}(\mathbf{X}_0 \mathbf{q}_i) = \sum_{i=1}^d \lambda_i = \operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{S}) = \frac{1}{N-1}\|\mathbf{X}_0\|_F^2$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove via Lagrange multipliers that maximizing $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{u}, \lambda) = \mathbf{u}^T \mathbf{S}\mathbf{u} - \lambda(\mathbf{u}^T \mathbf{u} - 1)$ yields $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{u} = \lambda \mathbf{u}$, and that the maximum value is strictly the largest eigenvalue $\lambda_1$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Given 4 centered 2D data points $\begin{pmatrix} -1.5 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix} -0.5 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix} 0.5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix} 1.5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$, compute the sample covariance matrix $\mathbf{S}$, find its eigenvalues and principal components $\mathbf{q}_1, \mathbf{q}_2$, and calculate the percentage of variance explained by $\mathbf{q}_1$.
  - [ ] **Proof 3**: Prove that $\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{S}) = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^d \sigma_j^2(\mathbf{X}_0)$ where $\sigma_j$ are the singular values of $\mathbf{X}_0$.
