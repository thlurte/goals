### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §1.1–1.6 (*Vector Geometry, 4 Perspectives of Matrix Multiplication & $LU$ Factorization*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The 4 Fundamental Perspectives of Matrix Multiplication $C = AB$**:
     * **Column Perspective**: Each column of $C$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$: $\mathbf{c}_j = \mathbf{A} \mathbf{b}_j$.
     * **Row Perspective**: Each row of $C$ is a linear combination of the rows of $B$: $\mathbf{c}_i^T = \mathbf{a}_i^T \mathbf{B}$.
     * **Outer Product Sum**: $C$ is the sum of $k$ rank-1 outer product matrices: $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B} = \sum_{k=1}^r \mathbf{a}_k \mathbf{b}_k^T$.
     * **Inner Product Matrix**: Entry $c_{ij}$ is the dot product of row $i$ of $A$ and column $j$ of $B$: $c_{ij} = \mathbf{a}_i^T \mathbf{b}_j$.
  2. **The $LU$ Factorization Without Pivoting**:
     $$\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{L} \mathbf{U} \quad \text{where } \mathbf{L} \text{ is unit lower triangular and } \mathbf{U} \text{ is upper triangular}$$
     * The elimination multipliers $\ell_{ij} = \frac{a_{ij}^{(j)}}{a_{jj}^{(j)}}$ fit directly into the subdiagonal entries of $\mathbf{L}$.
  3. **Solving $Ax = b$ in $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ via Two Triangular Solves**:
     * Step 1 (Forward solve): $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{b}$
     * Step 2 (Back solve): $\mathbf{U}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{y}$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove algebraic equivalence between the inner product definition $C_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^p A_{ik} B_{kj}$ and the outer product expansion $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B} = \sum_{k=1}^p \mathbf{A}_{:, k} \mathbf{B}_{k, :}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Compute the complete $LU$ factorization for $\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 4 & -6 & 0 \\ -2 & 7 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ step-by-step, recording each elementary elimination matrix $\mathbf{E}_{21}, \mathbf{E}_{31}, \mathbf{E}_{32}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Solve $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -2 \\ 9 \end{pmatrix}$ using your computed $\mathbf{L}$ and $\mathbf{U}$ factors via forward-backward substitution.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §2.1–2.2 (*Vector Spaces, Subspaces, Column Space $C(A)$ & Nullspace $N(A)$*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Vector Subspaces**:
     * A subset $\mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is a linear subspace $\iff$ it satisfies two closure rules:
       $$\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in \mathcal{S} \implies \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v} \in \mathcal{S}, \quad \mathbf{u} \in \mathcal{S}, c \in \mathbb{R} \implies c\mathbf{u} \in \mathcal{S}$$
  2. **The Column Space $C(A) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$**:
     * The linear span of all $n$ columns of $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$.
     * System $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ has a valid solution $\iff \mathbf{b} \in C(\mathbf{A})$.
  3. **The Nullspace $N(A) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ & Special Solutions**:
     * The set of all vectors $\mathbf{x}$ satisfying the homogeneous equation $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$.
     * Special solutions are computed by reducing $\mathbf{A} \to \mathbf{R}$ (RREF), setting each free variable to $1$ (with other free variables $0$), and solving for pivot variables.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove that the nullspace $N(\mathbf{A}) = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}\}$ is a linear subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$ by directly verifying the addition and scalar multiplication closure properties.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: For matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 & 4 \\ 3 & 8 & 6 & 16 \end{pmatrix}$, find the Reduced Row Echelon Form $\mathbf{R}$, identify pivot/free variables, and write the basis vectors of $N(\mathbf{A})$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For what exact conditions on vector $\mathbf{b} = \langle b_1, b_2, b_3 \rangle^T$ does $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 6 \\ 3 & 9 \end{pmatrix} \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ possess a solution? Describe the geometry of $C(\mathbf{A})$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §2.3–2.4 (*Linear Independence, Basis, Dimension & The Rank-Nullity Theorem*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Linear Independence**:
     * Vectors $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_k\}$ are linearly independent $\iff$ the only solution to $\sum c_i \mathbf{v}_i = \mathbf{0}$ is $c_1 = \dots = c_k = 0$.
     * Columns of $\mathbf{A}$ are linearly independent $\iff N(\mathbf{A}) = \{\mathbf{0}\}$.
  2. **Basis & Dimension**:
     * A basis for a vector space $V$ is a minimal spanning set (or maximal linearly independent set).
     * Every basis of $V$ contains the exact same number of vectors, defined as $\operatorname{dim}(V)$.
  3. **The Rank-Nullity Theorem (Fundamental Dimension Formula)**:
     * For any matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$:
       $$\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A}) + \operatorname{dim}(N(\mathbf{A})) = n \implies r + (n - r) = n$$
     * Number of pivot columns (rank $r$) plus number of free columns (nullity $n-r$) equals total columns $n$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove that any set of non-zero pairwise orthogonal vectors $\{\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_k\}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ ($\mathbf{u}_i \cdot \mathbf{u}_j = 0$ for $i \ne j$) is strictly linearly independent by taking the inner product of $\sum c_i \mathbf{u}_i = \mathbf{0}$ with each $\mathbf{u}_j$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove the Rank-Nullity Theorem by constructing the $n-r$ special solutions from the reduced row echelon form $\mathbf{R}$ and proving they form a basis for $N(\mathbf{A})$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Find a basis for and calculate the dimension of the subspace $\mathcal{W} \subset \mathbb{R}^4$ defined by the linear constraint $x_1 + 2x_2 - x_3 + 3x_4 = 0$.
