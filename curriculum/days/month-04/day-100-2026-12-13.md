### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §6.4–6.5 (*MGF of Normal Distributions, Linear Combinations & Chi-Square Distributions*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Sums of Independent Normal Random Variables**:
     * If $X_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_1, \sigma_1^2)$ and $X_2 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_2, \sigma_2^2)$ are independent:
       $$M_{X_1+X_2}(t) = M_{X_1}(t) M_{X_2}(t) = \exp\left( (\mu_1 + \mu_2)t + \frac{1}{2}(\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2)t^2 \right)$$
       $\implies X_1 + X_2 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_1 + \mu_2, \sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2)$.
     * General Linear Combination: $\sum_{i=1}^n c_i X_i \sim \mathcal{N}\left( \sum c_i \mu_i, \sum c_i^2 \sigma_i^2 \right)$.
  2. **The Chi-Square Distribution $\chi^2(k)$**:
     * If $Z_1, \dots, Z_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ are i.i.d., $V = \sum_{i=1}^k Z_i^2 \sim \chi^2(k)$.
     * **MGF**: $M_V(t) = (1 - 2t)^{-k/2} \implies E[V] = k, \operatorname{Var}(V) = 2k$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove using MGF factorization that any linear combination $\sum_{i=1}^n c_i X_i$ of independent normals $X_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_i, \sigma_i^2)$ is strictly normal $\mathcal{N}(\sum c_i \mu_i, \sum c_i^2 \sigma_i^2)$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the MGF of $Z^2$ for standard normal $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$: $M_{Z^2}(t) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{tz^2} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-z^2/2}dz = (1 - 2t)^{-1/2}$ for $t < 1/2$. Deduce the MGF and mean/variance of $\chi^2(k)$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In high-dimensional spherical vector spaces $\mathbb{R}^d$, the squared Euclidean distance between two independent random unit vectors behaves as $D^2 \sim \frac{2}{d} \chi^2(d)$. Compute $E[D^2] = 2$ and $\operatorname{Var}(D^2) = \frac{4}{d}$, proving the concentration of pairwise distances at $\sqrt{2}$ as $d \to \infty$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §6.6 (*Gamma, Beta, and Cauchy Distributions & Pathological Moments*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Gamma Distribution $\operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha, \lambda)$**:
     $$f_X(x) = \frac{\lambda^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)} x^{\alpha - 1} e^{-\lambda x} \quad (x > 0) \implies E[X] = \frac{\alpha}{\lambda}, \operatorname{Var}(X) = \frac{\alpha}{\lambda^2}$$
     * **Gamma Function Property**: $\Gamma(\alpha) = \int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1} e^{-t} dt \implies \Gamma(\alpha+1) = \alpha \Gamma(\alpha)$, $\Gamma(n) = (n-1)!$.
  2. **The Beta Distribution $\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)$**:
     $$f_X(x) = \frac{1}{B(\alpha, \beta)} x^{\alpha-1} (1-x)^{\beta-1} \quad (x \in (0, 1)) \implies E[X] = \frac{\alpha}{\alpha + \beta}$$
  3. **The Cauchy Distribution & Failure of LLN**:
     $$f_X(x) = \frac{1}{\pi (1 + x^2)} \quad (x \in \mathbb{R})$$
     * $\int_{-\infty}^\infty |x| f_X(x) dx = \infty \implies E[X]$ is **strictly undefined**.
     * The average of $n$ i.i.d. Cauchy variables $\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ retains the exact same standard Cauchy distribution $\operatorname{Cauchy}(0, 1)$ with no variance reduction.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove $\Gamma(\alpha + 1) = \alpha \Gamma(\alpha)$ by applying integration by parts on $\int_0^\infty t^\alpha e^{-t}dt$. Conclude that $\Gamma(n) = (n-1)!$ for all integers $n \ge 1$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that the Cauchy distribution does not have a well-defined expectation by demonstrating that $\int_0^\infty x \frac{1}{\pi(1+x^2)}dx = \infty$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In 1-stable Locality-Sensitive Hashing, projection vectors are drawn from standard Cauchy $R_i \sim \operatorname{Cauchy}(0, 1)$. Use characteristic function $\phi(t) = e^{-|t|}$ to prove that $\langle \mathbf{R}, \mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v} \rangle \sim \|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}\|_1 \operatorname{Cauchy}(0, 1)$, enabling $L_1$ distance preservation.
