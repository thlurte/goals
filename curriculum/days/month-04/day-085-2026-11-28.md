### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §1.1–1.4 (*Sample Spaces, Events, Kolmogorov's Axioms & Union Bounds*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The 3 Kolmogorov Axioms of Probability**:
     * **Axiom 1 (Non-negativity)**: For any event $A \subseteq \Omega$, $P(A) \ge 0$.
     * **Axiom 2 (Normalization)**: $P(\Omega) = 1$.
     * **Axiom 3 (Countable Additivity)**: If $A_1, A_2, \dots$ is a sequence of disjoint events ($A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset$ for $i \ne j$), then:
       $$P\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^\infty A_i \right) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty P(A_i)$$
  2. **Core Derived Theorems**:
     * **Complement Rule**: $P(A^c) = 1 - P(A)$.
     * **Inclusion-Exclusion Principle**: $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$.
     * **Boole's Inequality (The Union Bound)**: For any arbitrary collection of events $A_1, \dots, A_n$:
       $$P\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i \right) \le \sum_{i=1}^n P(A_i)$$
     * **Continuity of Probability**: If $E_1 \subseteq E_2 \subseteq \dots$ and $E = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty E_n$, then $\lim_{n \to \infty} P(E_n) = P(E)$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove $P(\emptyset) = 0$ and $P(A^c) = 1 - P(A)$ directly from Kolmogorov's Axioms 2 and 3.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove Boole's inequality $P(\bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i) \le \sum_{i=1}^n P(A_i)$ by induction on $n$ using disjointification $B_1 = A_1, B_k = A_k \setminus \bigcup_{j=1}^{k-1} A_j$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In high-dimensional LSH with $K$ hash tables, let $E_i$ be the event that a false positive collision occurs in table $i$. Use Boole's inequality to derive an upper bound on the probability of retrieving at least one false positive candidate across all $K$ tables.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §1.5–1.6 (*Combinatorics: Permutations, Combinations $\binom{n}{k}$ & Binomial Theorem*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Permutations & Combinations**:
     * Number of $k$-permutations of $n$ distinct items: $P(n, k) = \frac{n!}{(n-k)!}$.
     * Number of $k$-element subsets (combinations): $\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$.
  2. **The Binomial Theorem & Pascal's Identity**:
     $$(x + y)^n = \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} x^k y^{n-k}$$
     * **Pascal's Identity**: $\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n-1}{k-1} + \binom{n-1}{k}$.
     * **Total Subsets**: $\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} = (1 + 1)^n = 2^n$.
  3. **Multinomial Partitions**:
     $$\binom{n}{n_1, n_2, \dots, n_r} = \frac{n!}{n_1! n_2! \dots n_r!} \quad \text{where } \sum_{i=1}^r n_i = n$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove Pascal's identity $\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n-1}{k-1} + \binom{n-1}{k}$ both algebraically and by a combinatorial partition of subsets containing vs not containing a distinguished element $x_0$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove the Binomial Theorem by mathematical induction on $n$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In an IVF vector index with $C = 4{,}096$ inverted lists and multi-probe parameter $nprobe = 32$, compute the total number of candidate probing paths $\binom{4096}{32}$ using Stirling's approximation $\ln n! \approx n \ln n - n + \frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi n)$.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §2.1–2.3 (*Conditional Probability, Multiplication Rule & Law of Total Probability*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Conditional Probability & The Multiplication Rule**:
     * For any event $B$ with $P(B) > 0$:
       $$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$$
     * **Multiplication Rule**:
       $$P(A_1 \cap A_2 \cap \dots \cap A_n) = P(A_1) P(A_2 \mid A_1) P(A_3 \mid A_1 \cap A_2) \dots P(A_n \mid A_1 \cap \dots \cap A_{n-1})$$
  2. **The Law of Total Probability**:
     * If events $A_1, \dots, A_n$ form a partition of sample space $\Omega$ ($A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset, \bigcup A_i = \Omega$), then for any event $B$:
       $$P(B) = \sum_{i=1}^n P(A_i \cap B) = \sum_{i=1}^n P(A_i) P(B \mid A_i)$$
  3. **Gambler's Ruin Difference Equation**:
     * State probability $p_i$ of reaching fortune $N$ starting from $i$:
       $$p_i = p \cdot p_{i+1} + q \cdot p_{i-1} \implies p_i = \frac{1 - (q/p)^i}{1 - (q/p)^N} \quad (\text{for } p \ne q)$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove the Law of Total Probability $P(B) = \sum_{i=1}^n P(A_i) P(B \mid A_i)$ using Kolmogorov's Axiom 3 and disjoint decomposition $B = \bigcup_{i=1}^n (B \cap A_i)$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the analytical solution to the Gambler's Ruin difference equation $p_i = p \cdot p_{i+1} + (1-p) \cdot p_{i-1}$ with boundary conditions $p_0 = 0$ and $p_N = 1$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In approximate vector search routing, a query visits cluster $C_1, C_2, C_3$ with probabilities $0.5, 0.3, 0.2$. The conditional recall given cluster visit is $0.98, 0.85, 0.60$. Compute the total expected query Recall@10.
