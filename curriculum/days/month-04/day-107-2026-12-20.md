### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §7.6–7.7 (*Covariance, Correlation & Cauchy-Schwarz Bound on $\rho$*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Covariance & Correlation Coefficient**:
     * $\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y) = E[(X - \mu_X)(Y - \mu_Y)] = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y]$.
     * Pearson Correlation: $\rho(X, Y) = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y}$.
  2. **The Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality on Random Variables**:
     * For any random variables with finite second moments:
       $$(\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y))^2 \le \sigma_X^2 \sigma_Y^2 \implies -1 \le \rho(X, Y) \le 1$$
     * **Quadratic Discriminant Proof**: Let $\tilde{X} = X - \mu_X, \tilde{Y} = Y - \mu_Y$. Consider $h(t) = E[(t \tilde{X} + \tilde{Y})^2] = t^2 \sigma_X^2 + 2t \operatorname{Cov}(X, Y) + \sigma_Y^2 \ge 0$. Since $h(t) \ge 0$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$, its discriminant $\Delta = 4(\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y))^2 - 4\sigma_X^2 \sigma_Y^2 \le 0$.
  3. **Linear Determinism**:
     * $|\rho(X, Y)| = 1 \iff Y = aX + b$ almost surely for non-zero constant $a$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the Cauchy-Schwarz bound $|\rho(X, Y)| \le 1$ using the non-negativity of the quadratic expectation $h(t) = E[(t \tilde{X} + \tilde{Y})^2] \ge 0$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that if $\rho(X, Y) = 1$, the minimum of $h(t)$ is zero, implying $P\left(Y - \mu_Y = \frac{\sigma_Y}{\sigma_X}(X - \mu_X)\right) = 1$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In ColBERT late-interaction token scoring, let $T_1, T_2$ be two query token embedding scores with variance $\sigma^2 = 1.0$ and cross-correlation $\rho = 0.85$. Compute $\operatorname{Var}(T_1 + T_2) = 3.70$, and compare against uncorrelated tokens ($\operatorname{Var} = 2.0$).

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §7.8 (*The Multivariate Normal Distribution $\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma})$ & Joint Gaussians*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Multivariate Normal Joint PDF**:
     $$f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2} \det(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})^{1/2}} \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}) \right)$$
     where $\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = E[(\mathbf{X} - \boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{X} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^T] \succ 0$ is symmetric positive definite.
  2. **Affine Transformation Invariance**:
     * If $\mathbf{X} \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma})$ and $\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{b}$, then $\mathbf{Y} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\mu} + \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{A}^T)$.
  3. **Zero Covariance Implies Mutual Independence**:
     * For jointly Gaussian variables, $\operatorname{Cov}(X_i, X_j) = 0 \iff X_i \perp X_j$.
     * The covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ diagonalizes, factorizing the joint PDF: $f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x}) = \prod_{i=1}^d f_{X_i}(x_i)$.
  4. **Asymptotic Random Matrix Theory & The Wigner Semicircle Law**:
     * For an $n \times n$ symmetric random matrix $\mathbf{W}$ with i.i.d. zero-mean, unit-variance entries ($w_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ for $i < j$):
     * Normalized matrix $\mathbf{M} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbf{W}$ has empirical spectral distribution converging to the **Wigner Semicircle Law**:
       $$\rho_{\text{sc}}(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2\pi R^2} \sqrt{4R^2 - \lambda^2} \quad \text{for } |\lambda| \le 2R \quad (R=1)$$
  5. **The Marchenko–Pastur Law (Sample Covariance Spectrum)**:
     * Let $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$ contain i.i.d. entries with mean 0 and variance $\sigma^2$.
     * Consider the sample covariance matrix $\mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{N} \mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$.
     * As $d, N \to \infty$ with aspect ratio $\gamma = d/N \in (0, 1]$, the eigenvalue density converges to the **Marchenko–Pastur distribution**:
       $$p_{\text{MP}}(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2\pi \sigma^2 \gamma \lambda} \sqrt{(\lambda_+ - \lambda)(\lambda - \lambda_-)} \quad \text{for } \lambda \in [\lambda_-, \lambda_+]$$
       where the hard spectral edges are given by:
       $$\lambda_{\pm} = \sigma^2 (1 \pm \sqrt{\gamma})^2$$
     * **Retrieval & Whitening Systems Impact**: Explains why real text embeddings (768-D) have noise eigenvalues below $\lambda_+$. Naive covariance inversion $\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}$ blows up noise eigenvalues near $\lambda_-$. To prevent retrieval collapse, whitening must use **Tikhonov ridge regularization**:
       $$\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\text{reg}}^{-1/2} = (\mathbf{S} + \epsilon \mathbf{I})^{-1/2}, \quad \epsilon \ge \lambda_+$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the multivariate MGF $M_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{t}) = E[\exp(\mathbf{t}^T \mathbf{X})] = \exp(\mathbf{t}^T \boldsymbol{\mu} + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{t}^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \mathbf{t})$ by linear transformation $\mathbf{X} = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1/2} \mathbf{Z} + \boldsymbol{\mu}$ where $\mathbf{Z} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that if $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is diagonal ($\sigma_{ij} = 0$ for $i \ne j$), the exponent in the multivariate normal PDF separates into a sum of 1D squares, proving that joint normality + zero correlation implies strict mutual statistical independence.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In high-dimensional representation spaces, let document embeddings follow $\mathbf{X} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma})$. Show that the Mahalanobis distance $d_M(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \sqrt{(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})}$ is equivalent to standard Euclidean distance under PCA whitening transformation $\mathbf{z} = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\mathbf{x}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 4**: In a 768-D embedding space ($d=768$), we estimate sample covariance using a batch of $N=3072$ random vectors ($\gamma = d/N = 0.25, \sigma^2 = 1$). Compute the exact theoretical Marchenko–Pastur support boundaries $[\lambda_-, \lambda_+]$:
    $$\lambda_- = (1 - 0.5)^2 = 0.25, \quad \lambda_+ = (1 + 0.5)^2 = 2.25$$
    Prove why any empirical eigenvalue $\lambda > 2.25$ represents true semantic signal (the BBP phase transition) rather than random noise.
