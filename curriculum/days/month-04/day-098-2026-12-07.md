### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §4.4–4.6 (*LOTUS, Variance, Covariance & Sum of Random Variables*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Variance & Standard Deviation**:
     * $\operatorname{Var}(X) = E\left[(X - E[X])^2\right] = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2 \ge 0$.
     * **Scaling Property**: $\operatorname{Var}(aX + b) = a^2 \operatorname{Var}(X) \implies \sigma_{aX+b} = |a|\sigma_X$.
  2. **Covariance & Correlation**:
     * $\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y) = E[(X - \mu_X)(Y - \mu_Y)] = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y]$.
     * $\rho(X, Y) = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y} \in [-1, 1]$.
  3. **Variance of Sums**:
     $$\operatorname{Var}\left( \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \right) = \sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{Var}(X_i) + 2 \sum_{1 \le i < j \le n} \operatorname{Cov}(X_i, X_j)$$
     * If $X_1, \dots, X_n$ are uncorrelated, variance is strictly additive: $\operatorname{Var}(\sum X_i) = \sum \operatorname{Var}(X_i)$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove $\operatorname{Var}(aX + b) = a^2 \operatorname{Var}(X)$ by expanding $E[(aX + b - E[aX + b])^2]$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the variance expansion formula $\operatorname{Var}(X + Y) = \operatorname{Var}(X) + \operatorname{Var}(Y) + 2\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y)$ from first principles.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Let $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ be a random vector with i.i.d. coordinates $X_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$. Let $S = \|\mathbf{x}\|_2^2 = \sum_{i=1}^d X_i^2$. Compute $E[S]$ and $\operatorname{Var}(S) = 2d$, and compute the coefficient of variation $\frac{\sigma_S}{E[S]} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{d}} \to 0$ as $d \to \infty$ (proving spherical shell concentration!).

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §4.7–4.9 (*The Poisson Distribution, Limit Theorems & The Law of Rare Events*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Poisson Distribution $\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$**:
     $$p_X(k) = \frac{\lambda^k e^{-\lambda}}{k!} \quad (k = 0, 1, 2, \dots)$$
     * **Mean & Variance Equality**: $E[X] = \lambda$, $\operatorname{Var}(X) = \lambda$.
  2. **The Poisson Limit Theorem (Law of Rare Events)**:
     * Let $X_n \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(n, p_n)$ where $n \to \infty$ and $p_n \to 0$ such that $n p_n = \lambda$ remains constant:
       $$\lim_{n \to \infty} \binom{n}{k} p_n^k (1 - p_n)^{n-k} = \frac{\lambda^k e^{-\lambda}}{k!}$$
  3. **Superposition of Poisson Processes**:
     * If $X_1 \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_1)$ and $X_2 \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_2)$ are independent:
       $$X_1 + X_2 \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2)$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove the Poisson Limit Theorem by expanding $\binom{n}{k} \left(\frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^k \left(1 - \frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^{n-k}$ into $\frac{\lambda^k}{k!} \left[\frac{n(n-1)\dots(n-k+1)}{n^k}\right] \left(1 - \frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^n \left(1 - \frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^{-k}$ and taking $n \to \infty$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove $E[X] = \lambda$ and $E[X(X-1)] = \lambda^2$ for $X \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$ using the series expansion of $e^\lambda$, and deduce $\operatorname{Var}(X) = \lambda$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: A vector search node receives queries arriving as a Poisson process with arrival rate $\lambda = 200\text{ queries/sec}$. Compute the probability that in a $50\text{ ms}$ processing window ($\mu = 10$), the queue receives exactly 10 queries, and the probability of a burst exceeding 18 queries.
