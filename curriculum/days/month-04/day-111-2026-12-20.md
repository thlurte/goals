### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §7.1–7.2 (*Markov's Inequality, Chebyshev's Inequality & Cantelli's Bound*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Markov's Inequality**:
     * For any non-negative random variable $X \ge 0$ and any constant $a > 0$:
       $$P(X \ge a) \le \frac{E[X]}{a}$$
     * **Indicator Proof**: Since $a \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{X \ge a\}} \le X$, taking expectations gives $a P(X \ge a) \le E[X]$.
  2. **Chebyshev's Inequality**:
     * For any random variable $X$ with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$:
       $$P(|X - \mu| \ge k\sigma) \le \frac{1}{k^2}$$
     * Apply Markov's inequality to $Y = (X - \mu)^2$ with threshold $a = (k\sigma)^2$.
  3. **Cantelli's One-Sided Inequality**:
     * For any $a > 0$:
       $$P(X - \mu \ge a) \le \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma^2 + a^2}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive Markov's inequality $P(X \ge a) \le \frac{E[X]}{a}$ using the indicator inequality $a \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{X \ge a\}} \le X$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive Cantelli's one-sided bound $P(X - \mu \ge a) \le \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma^2 + a^2}$ by applying Markov's inequality to $(X - \mu + u)^2$ for $u > 0$ and minimizing the resulting upper bound $\frac{\sigma^2 + u^2}{(a + u)^2}$ with optimal choice $u^* = \sigma^2 / a$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: A vector search query engine has an average latency of $E[T] = 4.0\text{ ms}$ with standard deviation $\sigma = 1.5\text{ ms}$. Use Cantelli's inequality to find an upper bound on the probability that a query experiences tail latency exceeding $20\text{ ms}$ ($P(T \ge 20)$).

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §7.3–7.4 (*The Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN) & Convergence in Probability*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Sample Mean of i.i.d. Random Variables**:
     * Let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be i.i.d. with mean $\mu$ and finite variance $\sigma^2$.
     * Sample Mean: $\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \implies E[\bar{X}_n] = \mu, \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}_n) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}$.
  2. **The Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN)**:
     * For any arbitrary tolerance $\epsilon > 0$:
       $$\lim_{n \to \infty} P\left( |\bar{X}_n - \mu| \ge \epsilon \right) = 0$$
     * **Chebyshev Proof**: $P\left(|\bar{X}_n - \mu| \ge \epsilon\right) \le \frac{\operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}_n)}{\epsilon^2} = \frac{\sigma^2}{n \epsilon^2} \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} 0$.
  3. **Convergence in Probability**:
     * A sequence of random variables $Y_n$ converges in probability to $c$ ($Y_n \xrightarrow{p} c$) $\iff \forall \epsilon > 0, \lim_{n \to \infty} P(|Y_n - c| \ge \epsilon) = 0$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the WLLN rigorously by applying Chebyshev's inequality to $\bar{X}_n$ with variance $\frac{\sigma^2}{n}$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove the Continuous Mapping Theorem: If $Y_n \xrightarrow{p} c$ and function $g$ is continuous at $c$, then $g(Y_n) \xrightarrow{p} g(c)$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In an empirical vector search benchmark, we estimate mean query latency $\mu$ by taking the average $\bar{T}_n$ over $n$ independent queries. If latency variance is $\sigma^2 = 4\text{ ms}^2$, how many queries $n$ must be executed so that $|\bar{T}_n - \mu| < 0.1\text{ ms}$ with confidence at least $95\%$?

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §7.5 (*The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) & Box-Muller Transformation*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Central Limit Theorem (CLT)**:
     * Let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be i.i.d. random variables with mean $\mu$ and finite variance $\sigma^2 > 0$.
     * The normalized sum converges in distribution to the standard Gaussian:
       $$Z_n = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n X_i - n\mu}{\sigma \sqrt{n}} = \frac{\bar{X}_n - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$$
     * **MGF Proof**: Expanding standardized variable $Y = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}$ gives $M_Y(t) = 1 + \frac{t^2}{2} + o(t^2)$.
       $$M_{Z_n}(t) = \left[ M_Y\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right) \right]^n = \left[ 1 + \frac{t^2}{2n} + o\left(\frac{t^2}{n}\right) \right]^n \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} e^{t^2/2}$$
  2. **The Box-Muller Transformation**:
     * Given independent uniform variables $U_1, U_2 \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)$:
       $$Z_0 = \sqrt{-2 \ln U_1} \cos(2\pi U_2), \quad Z_1 = \sqrt{-2 \ln U_1} \sin(2\pi U_2)$$
       Then $Z_0, Z_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ are strictly independent standard Gaussian variables.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the Central Limit Theorem limit $\lim_{n \to \infty} [M_Y(t/\sqrt{n})]^n = e^{t^2/2}$ by taking the natural logarithm and applying L'Hôpital's rule.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the Box-Muller transformation using the 2D polar transformation $(r, \theta) = (\sqrt{-2\ln u_1}, 2\pi u_2)$ with Jacobian determinant $|J| = \frac{1}{2\pi} r e^{-r^2/2}$, proving $f(z_0, z_1) = \frac{1}{2\pi} e^{-(z_0^2 + z_1^2)/2}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In high-dimensional random hyperplanes with dimension $d = 1{,}024$, let $S = \sum_{i=1}^d X_i Y_i$ where $X_i, Y_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ are independent Rademacher coin flips ($E[S] = 0, \operatorname{Var}(S) = d$). Use the CLT to compute the probability that $|S| \ge 64$ ($P(|Z| \ge 2) \approx 0.0455$).
