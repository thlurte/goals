### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Elements of Information Theory* — Thomas M. Cover & Joy A. Thomas
* **Sections**: §2.4–2.6 (*Mutual Information, Information Identities & Symmetry*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Mutual Information Definition**:
     * Reduction in the uncertainty of $X$ due to knowledge of $Y$:
       $$I(X; Y) = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}, y \in \mathcal{Y}} p(x, y) \log_2 \frac{p(x, y)}{p(x) p(y)}$$
  2. **Equivalence to Relative Entropy**:
     $$I(X; Y) = D_{KL}(p(x, y) \parallel p(x) p(y)) \ge 0$$
     * $I(X; Y) = 0 \iff X$ and $Y$ are statistically independent.
  3. **Fundamental Entropy Identities**:
     $$I(X; Y) = H(X) - H(X \mid Y) = H(Y) - H(Y \mid X) = H(X) + H(Y) - H(X, Y)$$
     * Self-Information: $I(X; X) = H(X)$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the identity $I(X; Y) = H(X) + H(Y) - H(X, Y)$ by splitting $\log_2 \frac{p(x, y)}{p(x)p(y)} = \log_2 p(x, y) - \log_2 p(x) - \log_2 p(y)$ inside the joint expectation.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove non-negativity $I(X; Y) \ge 0$ using Gibbs' inequality on $D_{KL}(p(x, y) \parallel p(x)p(y))$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For bivariate normal variables $(X, Y) \sim \mathcal{N}\left(\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix}1 & \rho \\ \rho & 1\end{bmatrix}\right)$, derive the differential mutual information $I(X; Y) = -\frac{1}{2} \ln(1 - \rho^2)$. Prove that $I(X; Y) \to \infty$ as $|\rho| \to 1$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Elements of Information Theory* — Thomas M. Cover & Joy A. Thomas
* **Sections**: §3.1–3.3 (*Cross-Entropy, KL Divergence Equivalence & Maximum Likelihood Connection*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Cross-Entropy Definition**:
     $$H(P, Q) = -\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} P(x) \log_2 Q(x) = E_P\left[-\log_2 Q(X)\right]$$
  2. **Fundamental Cross-Entropy Decomposition**:
     $$H(P, Q) = H(P) + D_{KL}(P \parallel Q)$$
     * **Proof**:
       $$H(P) + D_{KL}(P \parallel Q) = -\sum P(x) \log P(x) + \sum P(x) \log \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} = -\sum P(x) \log Q(x) = H(P, Q)$$
  3. **Equivalence to Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)**:
     * Let $\hat{P}_{\text{data}} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \delta_{x_i}$ be the empirical data distribution.
     * $$H(\hat{P}_{\text{data}}, Q_\theta) = -\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \log Q_\theta(x_i) = -\frac{1}{n} \ell(\theta)$$
     * Minimizing cross-entropy $\iff$ minimizing $D_{KL}(\hat{P} \parallel Q_\theta) \iff$ maximizing log-likelihood $\ell(\theta)$!
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the identity $H(P, Q) = H(P) + D_{KL}(P \parallel Q)$ by writing $D_{KL}(P \parallel Q) = \sum P(x) \log P(x) - \sum P(x) \log Q(x)$ and rearranging terms.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that minimizing empirical cross-entropy loss over a discrete dataset is mathematically equivalent to finding the MLE $\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}} = \arg\max_\theta \sum \ln Q_\theta(x_i)$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In vector classifier training with $C = 1000$ classes and true one-hot target distribution $P = \delta_{k}$, let model output predict $Q(k) = 0.85$. Calculate the cross-entropy $H(P, Q) = -\ln(0.85) = 0.1625\text{ nats}$ and verify that $D_{KL}(P \parallel Q) = 0.1625\text{ nats}$ since target entropy $H(P) = 0$.
