### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Statistical Inference* — George Casella & Roger L. Berger / *All of Statistics* — Larry Wasserman
* **Sections**: §1.1–1.3 (*Point Estimation, Bessel's Correction & MSE Decomposition*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Point Estimators & Unbiasedness**:
     * An estimator $\hat{\theta} = W(X_1, \dots, X_n)$ is unbiased if $\operatorname{Bias}(\hat{\theta}) = E[\hat{\theta}] - \theta = 0$.
     * Sample Mean: $\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \implies E[\bar{X}_n] = \mu, \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}_n) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}$.
  2. **Bessel's Correction for Sample Variance**:
     * Naive estimator $S_n^2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X}_n)^2$ is biased: $E[S_n^2] = \frac{n-1}{n} \sigma^2$.
     * Unbiased Sample Variance:
       $$S^2 = \frac{1}{n-1} \sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X}_n)^2 \implies E[S^2] = \sigma^2$$
  3. **Mean Squared Error (MSE) Decomposition**:
     $$\operatorname{MSE}(\hat{\theta}) = E\left[(\hat{\theta} - \theta)^2\right] = \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\theta}) + \left(\operatorname{Bias}(\hat{\theta})\right)^2$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive Bessel's correction from first principles: Expand $\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X}_n)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2 - n \bar{X}_n^2$, use $E[X_i^2] = \sigma^2 + \mu^2$ and $E[\bar{X}_n^2] = \frac{\sigma^2}{n} + \mu^2$, and subtract to prove $E[\sum (X_i - \bar{X}_n)^2] = (n-1)\sigma^2$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the MSE decomposition $\operatorname{MSE}(\hat{\theta}) = \operatorname{Var}(\hat{\theta}) + \operatorname{Bias}(\hat{\theta})^2$ by adding and subtracting $E[\hat{\theta}]$ inside the expectation.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For Gaussian samples $X_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, use Cochran's theorem $\frac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma^2} \sim \chi^2(n-1)$ to calculate the exact variance of the sample variance $\operatorname{Var}(S^2) = \frac{2\sigma^4}{n-1}$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Statistical Inference* — George Casella & Roger L. Berger
* **Sections**: §2.1–2.3 (*Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) & Invariance Properties*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)**:
     * Likelihood: $L(\theta \mid \mathbf{x}) = \prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i \mid \theta)$. Log-Likelihood: $\ell(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln f(x_i \mid \theta)$.
     * Score Function: $S(\theta) = \nabla_\theta \ell(\theta) = \mathbf{0}$.
  2. **Canonical MLE Derivations**:
     * Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$: $\hat{\mu} = \bar{X}_n, \quad \hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X}_n)^2$.
     * Poisson $\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$: $\hat{\lambda} = \bar{X}_n$.
  3. **Zehna's Invariance Property of MLEs**:
     * If $\hat{\theta}$ is the MLE of parameter $\theta$, and $g(\theta)$ is any continuous transformation (not necessarily one-to-one), then:
       $$\widehat{g(\theta)}_{\text{MLE}} = g(\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}})$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the joint MLE $(\hat{\mu}, \hat{\sigma}^2)$ for $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ by taking partial derivatives of $\ell(\mu, \sigma^2) = -\frac{n}{2}\ln(2\pi\sigma^2) - \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \mu)^2$ and showing the Hessian is strictly negative definite at the stationary point.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove Zehna's Invariance Theorem by showing that $\sup_\tau L^*(\tau) = \sup_\tau \sup_{\theta \in g^{-1}(\tau)} L(\theta) = \sup_\theta L(\theta) = L(\hat{\theta})$, attaining its supremum at $\tau^* = g(\hat{\theta})$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In request arrival modeling for a vector search endpoint, query interarrival times follow an exponential distribution $\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda)$ with PDF $f(t) = \lambda e^{-\lambda t}$. Derive the MLE for $\lambda$ ($\hat{\lambda} = 1/\bar{T}$) and use the invariance property to write the MLE for the probability of tail burst delay $P(T \ge t_0) = e^{-\hat{\lambda} t_0}$.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Statistical Inference* — George Casella & Roger L. Berger
* **Sections**: §2.4–2.5 (*Fisher Information & Score Variance vs Hessian Equivalence*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Fisher Information Definition**:
     $$I(\theta) = E\left[ \left( \frac{\partial \ln f(X \mid \theta)}{\partial \theta} \right)^2 \right] = \operatorname{Var}\left( S(\theta) \right)$$
  2. **Equivalence Theorem (Regularity Conditions)**:
     * Assuming interchangeability of differentiation and integration:
       $$I(\theta) = -E\left[ \frac{\partial^2 \ln f(X \mid \theta)}{\partial \theta^2} \right]$$
     * **Derivation**: Differentiating $\int f(x \mid \theta)dx = 1$ twice with respect to $\theta$:
       $$\frac{d}{d\theta} \int f dx = \int \frac{\partial \ln f}{\partial \theta} f dx = E[S(\theta)] = 0$$
       $$\frac{d^2}{d\theta^2} \int f dx = \int \frac{\partial^2 \ln f}{\partial \theta^2} f dx + \int \left( \frac{\partial \ln f}{\partial \theta} \right)^2 f dx = 0 \implies E\left[ \frac{\partial^2 \ln f}{\partial \theta^2} \right] + I(\theta) = 0$$
  3. **Multi-Parameter Fisher Information Matrix**:
     $$[\mathbf{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta})]_{jk} = -E\left[ \frac{\partial^2 \ln f(X \mid \boldsymbol{\theta})}{\partial \theta_j \partial \theta_k} \right]$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the Fisher Information equivalence $I(\theta) = -E[\frac{\partial^2 \ln f}{\partial \theta^2}]$ step-by-step using the product rule on $\frac{\partial}{\partial \theta}(\frac{\partial f}{\partial \theta} \cdot \frac{1}{f} \cdot f)$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Compute the $2 \times 2$ Fisher Information matrix $\mathbf{I}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ for Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$:
    $$\mathbf{I}(\mu, \sigma^2) = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{\sigma^2} & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1}{2\sigma^4} \end{bmatrix}$$
    proving that $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ are asymptotically orthogonal and uncorrelated.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For Bernoulli distribution $\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$ with PDF $f(x \mid p) = p^x (1-p)^{1-x}$, compute $I(p) = \frac{1}{p(1-p)}$. Prove that Fisher Information tends to infinity as $p \to 0$ or $p \to 1$.
