### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §11.1–11.2 (*Discrete-Time Markov Chains & Transition Probability Matrices*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Markov Property**:
     * A stochastic process $\{X_n, n \ge 0\}$ with discrete state space $S$ satisfies the Markov property if:
       $$P(X_{n+1} = j \mid X_n = i, X_{n-1} = i_{n-1}, \dots, X_0 = i_0) = P(X_{n+1} = j \mid X_n = i) = p_{ij}$$
  2. **Transition Probability Matrix & Stochastic Properties**:
     * $P = [p_{ij}]_{i,j \in S}$ where $p_{ij} \ge 0$ and every row sums to unity: $\sum_{j \in S} p_{ij} = 1 \iff P \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}$.
     * Spectral Property: The spectral radius $\rho(P) = 1$, and $\lambda = 1$ is an eigenvalue with right eigenvector $\mathbf{1} = [1, \dots, 1]^T$.
  3. **State Distribution Evolution**:
     * Let $\mathbf{p}(n) = [P(X_n = 1), \dots, P(X_n = |S|)]$. Then:
       $$\mathbf{p}(n) = \mathbf{p}(n-1) P = \mathbf{p}(0) P^n$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove by mathematical induction that the $n$-step transition probability satisfies $P(X_n = j \mid X_0 = i) = [P^n]_{ij}$, where $P^n$ is the standard matrix power.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Use Gershgorin's Circle Theorem to prove that for any row-stochastic matrix $P$, all eigenvalues $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ lie in the unit disc $|\lambda| \le 1$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: A vector search cache replacement model has state space $S = \{1: \text{L1 Hit}, 2: \text{L2 Hit}, 3: \text{DRAM Miss}\}$ with transition matrix:
    $$P = \begin{bmatrix} 0.80 & 0.15 & 0.05 \\ 0.60 & 0.30 & 0.10 \\ 0.40 & 0.40 & 0.20 \end{bmatrix}$$
    Compute $P^2$ explicitly and calculate the probability that a query starting in L1 experiences a DRAM miss on step 2 ($[P^2]_{13} = 0.8(0.05) + 0.15(0.10) + 0.05(0.20) = 0.065$).

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §11.3 (*Classification of States: Recurrence, Transience & Absorbing Chains*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Recurrence vs Transience**:
     * First Return Probability: $f_{ii} = P(\exists n \ge 1 : X_n = i \mid X_0 = i)$.
     * Recurrent State: $f_{ii} = 1 \iff \sum_{n=1}^\infty p_{ii}^{(n)} = \infty$ (visited infinitely often with prob 1).
     * Transient State: $f_{ii} < 1 \iff \sum_{n=1}^\infty p_{ii}^{(n)} = \frac{f_{ii}}{1 - f_{ii}} < \infty$.
     * **Class Property**: If $i \leftrightarrow j$ and $i$ is recurrent, then $j$ is recurrent.
  2. **Periodicity**:
     * Period $d(i) = \operatorname{gcd}\{n \ge 1 : p_{ii}^{(n)} > 0\}$. State is aperiodic if $d(i) = 1$.
  3. **Absorbing Chains & The Fundamental Matrix**:
     * Arranging transition matrix in canonical block form:
       $$P = \begin{bmatrix} Q & R \\ 0 & I \end{bmatrix}$$
     * Fundamental Matrix: $N = (I - Q)^{-1} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty Q^k$.
     * $N_{ij}$ is the exact expected number of visits to transient state $j$ starting from $i$ before absorption.
     * Expected time to absorption from state $i$: $\mathbf{t} = N \mathbf{1}$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove that recurrence is a class property: Given $p_{ij}^{(m)} > 0$ and $p_{ji}^{(k)} > 0$, show that $p_{jj}^{(m+n+k)} \ge p_{ji}^{(k)} p_{ii}^{(n)} p_{ij}^{(m)}$, and sum over $n$ to prove $\sum_{n} p_{jj}^{(n)} = \infty$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the fundamental matrix equation $N = (I - Q)^{-1}$ by summing the geometric series of expectations $E[\sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbf{1}_{\{X_k = j\}} \mid X_0 = i] = \sum_{k=0}^\infty [Q^k]_{ij}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In graph beam search early stopping, transient states $S_T = \{1: \text{Exploring}, 2: \text{Refining}\}$ transition to absorbing state $3$ (Convergence) with submatrix:
    $$Q = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 & 0.2 \\ 0.1 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}$$
    Compute $I - Q$, its inverse $N = (I - Q)^{-1}$, and the expected number of steps to convergence starting from Exploring ($t_1 = N_{11} + N_{12}$).

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §11.4 (*Stationary Distributions & Left Eigenvector Formulation*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Stationary Distribution Definition**:
     * A probability row vector $\boldsymbol{\pi} = [\pi_1, \dots, \pi_k]$ is stationary if:
       $$\boldsymbol{\pi} P = \boldsymbol{\pi} \quad \text{subject to } \sum_{i=1}^k \pi_i = 1, \pi_i \ge 0$$
     * Formulated as a homogeneous linear system: $\boldsymbol{\pi} (I - P) = \mathbf{0}^T$.
  2. **Perron-Frobenius Theorem for Stochastic Matrices**:
     * For an irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain with transition matrix $P$:
       * $\lambda_1 = 1$ is a simple eigenvalue with strictly positive left eigenvector $\boldsymbol{\pi} > 0$.
       * All other eigenvalues satisfy $|\lambda_i| < 1$.
  3. **Mean Recurrence Time**:
     * For any recurrent state $i \in S$, the expected number of steps until first return satisfies:
       $$E[T_{ii}] = \frac{1}{\pi_i}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove that $\operatorname{det}(I - P) = 0$ for any row-stochastic matrix $P$ by showing that $(I - P) \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}$, ensuring the null space of $(I - P)^T$ is non-trivial and non-zero stationary vectors exist.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that for a symmetric stochastic matrix ($p_{ij} = p_{ji}$ for all $i, j$), the uniform distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi} = \left[ \frac{1}{k}, \dots, \frac{1}{k} \right]$ is always the unique stationary distribution.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Find the unique stationary distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ for the 3-state vector cache transition matrix:
    $$P = \begin{bmatrix} 0.7 & 0.2 & 0.1 \\ 0.4 & 0.5 & 0.1 \\ 0.2 & 0.3 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}$$
    Solve the augmented linear system and compute the mean return time to DRAM Miss state 3 ($E[T_{33}] = 1/\pi_3$).
