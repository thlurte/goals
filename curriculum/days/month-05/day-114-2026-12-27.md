### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §10.4 (*The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) & Characteristic Function Proof*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Lévy's Continuity Theorem**:
     * A sequence of random variables $Z_n$ converges in distribution to $Z$ ($Z_n \xrightarrow{d} Z$) if and only if their characteristic functions converge pointwise: $\lim_{n \to \infty} \phi_{Z_n}(t) = \phi_Z(t)$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$.
  2. **The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) Derivation**:
     * Let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be i.i.d. random variables with $E[X_i] = 0, \operatorname{Var}(X_i) = 1$. Let $Z_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$.
     * Characteristic function expansion:
       $$\phi_X(u) = E\left[e^{i u X}\right] = 1 + i u E[X] - \frac{u^2}{2} E[X^2] + o(u^2) = 1 - \frac{u^2}{2} + o(u^2)$$
     * Applying independence across $n$ terms:
       $$\phi_{Z_n}(t) = \left[ \phi_X\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right) \right]^n = \left[ 1 - \frac{t^2}{2n} + o\left(\frac{t^2}{n}\right) \right]^n \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} e^{-t^2/2}$$
  3. **Berry-Esseen Theorem (Convergence Rate)**:
     * If $E[|X_1|^3] = \rho < \infty$:
       $$\sup_{z \in \mathbb{R}} \left| P(Z_n \le z) - \Phi(z) \right| \le \frac{C \rho}{\sigma^3 \sqrt{n}} \quad (C < 0.4748)$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Write out the formal limit proof: Prove that $\lim_{n \to \infty} \left(1 - \frac{t^2}{2n} + \epsilon_n(t)\right)^n = e^{-t^2/2}$ where $\epsilon_n(t) = o(1/n)$ by taking logarithms.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Use the CLT to prove that the sample median $\tilde{X}_n$ of $n$ i.i.d. continuous observations with median $\mu$ and density $f(\mu) > 0$ satisfies $\sqrt{n}(\tilde{X}_n - \mu) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(0, \frac{1}{4 f(\mu)^2}\right)$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In high-dimensional random dot products $S = \sum_{i=1}^d X_i Y_i$ where $X_i, Y_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ are unbiased random coin flips, apply the Berry-Esseen bound with $\rho = E[|X_1 Y_1|^3] = 1$ to prove that for dimension $d = 512$, the maximum CDF approximation error from normal distribution is bounded by $\frac{0.4748}{\sqrt{512}} \approx 0.021$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §10.5 (*Statistical Error Bounds & Non-Asymptotic Confidence Intervals*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Asymptotic Confidence Intervals (CLT)**:
     * By the Central Limit Theorem, the $1-\alpha$ confidence interval for unknown mean $\mu$ is:
       $$\mu \in \left[ \bar{X}_n - z_{\alpha/2} \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}}, \bar{X}_n + z_{\alpha/2} \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} \right]$$
       where $S_n = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X}_n)^2}$ is the sample standard deviation.
  2. **Non-Asymptotic Distribution-Free Confidence Intervals (Hoeffding)**:
     * For any bounded random variables $X_i \in [a, b]$, setting Hoeffding's bound $2\exp\left(-\frac{2n\epsilon^2}{(b-a)^2}\right) = \delta$ guarantees that with probability at least $1-\delta$:
       $$\mu \in \left[ \bar{X}_n - (b-a)\sqrt{\frac{\ln(2/\delta)}{2n}}, \bar{X}_n + (b-a)\sqrt{\frac{\ln(2/\delta)}{2n}} \right]$$
       * Valid for any sample size $n$, regardless of the underlying distribution!
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the non-asymptotic confidence interval width $\epsilon = (b-a)\sqrt{\frac{\ln(2/\delta)}{2n}}$ by algebraic inversion of Hoeffding's two-sided tail inequality.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the Student's $t$-statistic distribution $T = \frac{\bar{X}_n - \mu}{S_n / \sqrt{n}} \sim t_{n-1}$ for Gaussian samples using Cochran's Theorem ($S_n^2 \sim \frac{\sigma^2}{n-1}\chi^2(n-1)$ independent of $\bar{X}_n$).
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In an automated GPU kernel benchmark over $n = 1{,}000$ queries, empirical query latency is $\bar{T} = 0.20\text{ ms}$ with bounded max latency $1.0\text{ ms}$ ($b-a = 1.0$) and sample std $S_n = 0.05\text{ ms}$. Compute both the asymptotic $99\%$ confidence interval ($z_{0.005} = 2.576$) and the non-asymptotic distribution-free $99\%$ Hoeffding interval ($\delta = 0.01$).
