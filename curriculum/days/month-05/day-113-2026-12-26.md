### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §10.1 (*Moment Generating Inequalities & Chernoff Bounds*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Chernoff Bound**:
     * For any random variable $X$ and any threshold $a$:
       $$P(X \ge a) = P(e^{tX} \ge e^{ta}) \le \frac{E[e^{tX}]}{e^{ta}} = e^{-ta} M_X(t) \quad (\forall t > 0)$$
     * Taking the infimum over $t > 0$ yields the sharpest exponential tail bound:
       $$P(X \ge a) \le \inf_{t > 0} \exp\left( -(ta - \ln M_X(t)) \right) = \exp\left( -I(a) \right)$$
       where $I(a) = \sup_{t > 0} [ta - \ln M_X(t)]$ is the Legendre-Fenchel convex conjugate (rate function).
  2. **Sub-Gaussian Tail of Rademacher Sums**:
     * Let $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ where $X_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ are independent Rademacher variables.
     * By Hoeffding's Lemma, $M_{X_i}(t) = \cosh(t) \le e^{t^2/2} \implies M_{S_n}(t) \le e^{n t^2 / 2}$.
     * Optimizing $t^* = a/n$ gives:
       $$P(S_n \ge a) \le \exp\left( -\frac{a^2}{2n} \right)$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the generic Chernoff bound $P(X \ge a) \le \inf_{t > 0} e^{-ta} M_X(t)$ by applying Markov's inequality to the monotonically increasing transformation $Y = e^{tX}$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove Hoeffding's Lemma $\cosh(t) \le e^{t^2/2}$ by expanding $\cosh(t) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{t^{2k}}{(2k)!}$ and comparing term-by-term with $e^{t^2/2} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{t^{2k}}{2^k k!}$ using $(2k)! \ge 2^k k!$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In random hyperplane LSH with $n = 128$ hash bits, let $X$ be the number of matching bits for two orthogonal vectors ($E[X] = 64$). Use the Chernoff bound to compute an upper bound on the false positive collision probability $P(X \ge 96) \le \exp(-2(32)^2 / 128) = e^{-16} \approx 1.12 \times 10^{-7}$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §10.2 (*Hoeffding's Lemma, Hoeffding's Inequality & Sub-Gaussian Bounded Sums*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Hoeffding's Lemma**:
     * Let $X$ be a zero-mean random variable strictly bounded in $[a, b]$ ($a \le X \le b, E[X] = 0$). Then:
       $$M_X(t) = E\left[e^{tX}\right] \le \exp\left( \frac{t^2 (b - a)^2}{8} \right)$$
  2. **Hoeffding's Inequality for Independent Bounded Sums**:
     * Let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be independent random variables where $a_i \le X_i \le b_i$. Let $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$.
     * For any positive deviation $\epsilon > 0$:
       $$P\left( S_n - E[S_n] \ge \epsilon \right) \le \exp\left( -\frac{2\epsilon^2}{\sum_{i=1}^n (b_i - a_i)^2} \right)$$
       $$P\left( |S_n - E[S_n]| \ge \epsilon \right) \le 2\exp\left( -\frac{2\epsilon^2}{\sum_{i=1}^n (b_i - a_i)^2} \right)$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive Hoeffding's Lemma by using the convexity of $e^{tx}$ on $[a, b]$: $e^{tx} \le \frac{b-x}{b-a} e^{ta} + \frac{x-a}{b-a} e^{tb}$, taking expectations with $E[X] = 0$, setting $u = t(b-a), p = -a/(b-a)$, and bounding $\ln(1 - p + p e^u) - p u \le u^2 / 8$ via Taylor remainder theorem.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive Hoeffding's Inequality by multiplying the MGF bounds $M_{S_n}(t) = \prod_{i=1}^n M_{X_i}(t) \le \exp\left(\frac{t^2 \sum (b_i-a_i)^2}{8}\right)$ and minimizing $e^{-t\epsilon} M_{S_n}(t)$ at $t^* = \frac{4\epsilon}{\sum (b_i-a_i)^2}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In an automated vector retrieval test harness over $n = 5{,}000$ queries, each individual test result $R_i \in [0, 1]$ measures query accuracy. Use Hoeffding's inequality to prove that $P(|\bar{R}_n - \mu| \ge 0.01) \le 2\exp\left( -\frac{2(5000)(0.01)^2}{1} \right) = 2e^{-1} \approx 0.735$, and calculate the sample size $n$ needed for $<0.01$ confidence error ($n \ge 26{,}492$).

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Introduction to Probability* — Dimitri P. Bertsekas & John N. Tsitsiklis
* **Sections**: §10.3 (*Strong Law of Large Numbers (SLLN) & Borel-Cantelli Lemmas*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Almost Sure Convergence (Convergence with Probability 1)**:
     $$X_n \xrightarrow{a.s.} X \iff P\left( \left\{ \omega \in \Omega : \lim_{n \to \infty} X_n(\omega) = X(\omega) \right\} \right) = 1$$
     * Implies convergence in probability ($X_n \xrightarrow{a.s.} X \implies X_n \xrightarrow{p} X$), but the converse does not hold.
  2. **The First Borel-Cantelli Lemma**:
     * For any arbitrary sequence of events $E_1, E_2, \dots$:
       $$\sum_{n=1}^\infty P(E_n) < \infty \implies P\left( \limsup_{n \to \infty} E_n \right) = P(E_n \text{ infinitely often}) = 0$$
  3. **The Second Borel-Cantelli Lemma**:
     * If events $E_1, E_2, \dots$ are **mutually independent**:
       $$\sum_{n=1}^\infty P(E_n) = \infty \implies P\left( \limsup_{n \to \infty} E_n \right) = 1$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove the First Borel-Cantelli Lemma by defining $A_k = \bigcup_{n=k}^\infty E_n$, observing $\limsup E_n = \bigcap_{k=1}^\infty A_k$, bounding $P(A_k) \le \sum_{n=k}^\infty P(E_n)$, and taking the limit as $k \to \infty$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove the Strong Law of Large Numbers (SLLN) under the 4th moment assumption $E[X_i^4] < \infty$: Expand $E[(\bar{X}_n - \mu)^4] = \frac{n E[X_1^4] + 3n(n-1)\sigma^4}{n^4} = \mathcal{O}(1/n^2)$, apply Markov's inequality to show $P(|\bar{X}_n - \mu| \ge \epsilon) \le \frac{C}{\epsilon^4 n^2}$, and sum $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} < \infty$ to invoke Borel-Cantelli 1.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: In distributed consensus message replication, let $E_n$ be the event of a message checksum failure on transmission $n$. If $P(E_n) = \frac{1}{n^{1.2}}$, prove that checksum errors cease to occur after a finite number of transmissions almost surely.
