### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Section**: §13.8 (*Constrained Optimization & The Method of Lagrange Multipliers*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Lagrange Multiplier Theorem**:
     * To maximize or minimize $f(\mathbf{x})$ subject to equality constraint $g(\mathbf{x}) = k$:
       $$\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^*) = \lambda \nabla g(\mathbf{x}^*) \quad (\lambda \ne 0)$$
  2. **Geometric Proof of Tangency**:
     * Let $\mathbf{r}(t)$ be any smooth curve lying on the constraint surface $g(\mathbf{x}) = k$ with $\mathbf{r}(0) = \mathbf{x}^*$.
     * Since $g(\mathbf{r}(t)) = k$, taking the time derivative gives $\nabla g(\mathbf{x}^*) \cdot \mathbf{r}'(0) = 0$. Thus $\nabla g(\mathbf{x}^*)$ is orthogonal to the tangent space of the constraint surface.
     * If $\mathbf{x}^*$ is a local extremum of $f$, then $\frac{d}{dt} f(\mathbf{r}(t))\big|_{t=0} = \nabla f(\mathbf{x}^*) \cdot \mathbf{r}'(0) = 0$.
     * Since $\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^*)$ is orthogonal to every tangent vector $\mathbf{r}'(0)$, it must be collinear with the normal vector $\nabla g(\mathbf{x}^*)$.
  3. **The Lagrangian Formulation**:
     $$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}, \lambda) = f(\mathbf{x}) - \lambda(g(\mathbf{x}) - k) \implies \nabla_{\mathbf{x}, \lambda} \mathcal{L} = \mathbf{0}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Write out the complete orthogonality proof showing why $\nabla f \parallel \nabla g$ at constrained extrema.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Find the maximum and minimum values of $f(x, y, z) = x + 2y + 3z$ subject to the sphere constraint $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Maximize information entropy $H(p_1, \dots, p_n) = -\sum_{i=1}^n p_i \ln p_i$ subject to $\sum_{i=1}^n p_i = 1$ using Lagrange multipliers, proving the uniform distribution $p_i = \frac{1}{n}$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Section**: §13.8 (*Lagrange Multipliers with Multiple Constraints & Eigenvalue Problems*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Multiple Equality Constraints**:
     * To optimize $f(\mathbf{x})$ subject to $k$ constraints $g_1(\mathbf{x}) = c_1, \dots, g_k(\mathbf{x}) = c_k$:
       $$\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^*) = \lambda_1 \nabla g_1(\mathbf{x}^*) + \lambda_2 \nabla g_2(\mathbf{x}^*) + \dots + \lambda_k \nabla g_k(\mathbf{x}^*)$$
     * Geometric interpretation: The gradient $\nabla f$ lies in the linear subspace spanned by the constraint normals $\operatorname{span}\{\nabla g_1, \dots, \nabla g_k\}$.
  2. **The PCA Eigenvalue Connection via Lagrange Multipliers**:
     * Maximize quadratic variance $f(\mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{v}$ subject to unit norm constraint $g(\mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{v} = 1$:
       $$\nabla f(\mathbf{v}) = 2\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{v}, \quad \nabla g(\mathbf{v}) = 2\mathbf{v} \implies 2\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{v} = 2\lambda\mathbf{v} \iff \mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}$$
     * The optimal projection directions are the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive the two-constraint condition $\nabla f = \lambda_1 \nabla g_1 + \lambda_2 \nabla g_2$ by parameterizing the intersection curve $\mathcal{C} = \{g_1 = c_1\} \cap \{g_2 = c_2\}$ with velocity vector $\mathbf{v} = \nabla g_1 \times \nabla g_2$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Find the point on the intersection of the plane $x + y + z = 1$ and the cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ that is closest to the origin.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For a symmetric positive definite matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, maximize $\mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{A} \mathbf{x}$ subject to $\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 = 1$ and prove that the maximum value is exactly the spectral radius $\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{A})$.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §14.1–14.2 (*Double Integrals over Rectangular & General Regions, Fubini's Theorem*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Double Riemann Integral**:
     $$\iint_R f(x, y) dA = \lim_{\Delta x, \Delta y \to 0} \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^n f(x_i^*, y_j^*) \Delta x \Delta y$$
  2. **Fubini's Theorem for Iterated Integrals**:
     * If $f(x, y)$ is continuous on rectangle $R = [a, b] \times [c, d]$:
       $$\iint_R f(x, y) dA = \int_a^b \left( \int_c^d f(x, y) dy \right) dx = \int_c^d \left( \int_a^b f(x, y) dx \right) dy$$
  3. **Reversing Order of Integration on General Regions**:
     $$\int_{y=0}^1 \int_{x=y}^1 f(x, y) dx dy = \int_{x=0}^1 \int_{y=0}^x f(x, y) dy dx$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Evaluate $\iint_R x y e^{x^2 + y^2} dA$ on $R = [0, 1] \times [0, 1]$ along both integration orders ($dx dy$ and $dy dx$) to verify Fubini's equivalence.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Evaluate the seemingly impossible integral $\int_0^1 \int_y^1 e^{-x^2} dx dy$ by sketching the domain and reversing the order of integration to $dy dx$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Compute the volume bounded by the paraboloid $z = 4 - x^2 - y^2$ and the $xy$-plane ($z = 0$) using Cartesian double integration.
