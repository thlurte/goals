### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Topic**: *Matrix Calculus & Neural Network Backpropagation Foundations*
* **Core Text & References**: *Matrix Calculus for Deep Learning* (Parr & Howard) / *The Matrix Cookbook* (Petersen & Pedersen)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Matrix Derivative Definitions & Layout**:
     * For scalar loss $\mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}$ and matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$, the gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$ is defined element-wise by $\left[\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W}\right]_{ij} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_{ij}}$.
  2. **Differential & Trace Identities**:
     * $d\mathcal{L} = \operatorname{Tr}\left(\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W}\right)^T dW\right)$.
     * Cyclic property of trace: $\operatorname{Tr}(ABC) = \operatorname{Tr}(CAB) = \operatorname{Tr}(BCA)$.
     * Frobenius inner product: $\langle A, B \rangle = \operatorname{Tr}(A^T B)$.
  3. **Analytical Backprop for Batched Linear Layer ($Y = XW + b$)**:
     * Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times D_{\text{in}}}$, $W \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\text{in}} \times D_{\text{out}}}$, and $b \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times D_{\text{out}}}$. Given upstream gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times D_{\text{out}}}$:
       $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W} = X^T \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y}, \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial X} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y} W^T, \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial b} = \mathbf{1}_{B}^T \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W} = X^T \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y}$ step-by-step using index notation: show that $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_{ij}} = \sum_{b=1}^B \sum_{k=1}^{D_{\text{out}}} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y_{bk}} \frac{\partial Y_{bk}}{\partial W_{ij}} = \sum_{b=1}^B X_{bi} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y_{bj}} = \left[ X^T \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y} \right]_{ij}$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial X} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y} W^T$ using the trace differential identity: $d\mathcal{L} = \operatorname{Tr}\left(\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y}\right)^T dY\right) = \operatorname{Tr}\left(\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y}\right)^T (dX) W\right) = \operatorname{Tr}\left(W \left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y}\right)^T dX\right)$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For quadratic form $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x}$ where $A$ is non-symmetric, prove that $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^T)\mathbf{x}$. Verify that when $A$ is symmetric ($A = A^T$), this collapses to $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mathcal{L} = A \mathbf{x}$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Topic**: *Vector Jacobians, Activation Derivatives & Softmax-Cross-Entropy Gradients*
* **Core Text & References**: *Deep Learning* (Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville §6.5) / *Neural Network Gradient Foundations*
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Vector Jacobian Matrix**:
     * For mapping $\mathbf{f}: \mathbb{R}^N \to \mathbb{R}^M$, the Jacobian $J \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$ is $J_{ij} = \frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}$.
     * For element-wise activations $\mathbf{y} = \sigma(\mathbf{z})$ (e.g. ReLU, GELU, SwiGLU), $J$ is strictly **diagonal**: $J = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma'(z_1), \dots, \sigma'(z_N))$.
  2. **The Softmax Jacobian Derivation**:
     * For $s_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{k=1}^C e^{z_k}}$, the full dense Jacobian is:
       $$\frac{\partial s_i}{\partial z_j} = s_i (\delta_{ij} - s_j) = \begin{cases} s_i(1 - s_i) & \text{if } i = j \\ -s_i s_j & \text{if } i \neq j \end{cases}$$
       In matrix form: $J_{\text{softmax}} = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{s}) - \mathbf{s} \mathbf{s}^T$.
  3. **The Softmax-Cross-Entropy Miracle**:
     * For categorical cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} = -\sum_{i=1}^C y_i \ln s_i$ where $\mathbf{y}$ is one-hot ($\sum y_i = 1$):
       $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}}{\partial z_j} = s_j - y_j \iff \nabla_{\mathbf{z}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} = \mathbf{s} - \mathbf{y}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive $\frac{\partial s_i}{\partial z_j} = s_i(\delta_{ij} - s_j)$ using the quotient rule: $\frac{d}{dz_j} \left( \frac{e^{z_i}}{\Sigma} \right) = \frac{\frac{\partial e^{z_i}}{\partial z_j}\Sigma - e^{z_i} \frac{\partial \Sigma}{\partial z_j}}{\Sigma^2}$, noting that $\frac{\partial \Sigma}{\partial z_j} = e^{z_j}$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the combined gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{z}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} = \mathbf{s} - \mathbf{y}$ by chaining upstream loss gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial s_i} = -\frac{y_i}{s_i}$ through the Softmax Jacobian: show that $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_j} = \sum_{i=1}^C \left( -\frac{y_i}{s_i} \right) s_i (\delta_{ij} - s_j) = -y_j + s_j \sum_{i=1}^C y_i = s_j - y_j$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Compute the exact analytical derivative of the **SwiGLU** activation $\text{SwiGLU}(x, g) = \text{Swish}(x) \cdot g = (x \cdot \sigma(\beta x)) \cdot g$ with respect to inputs $x$ and gate $g$.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Topic**: *Attention Mechanism Matrix Calculus (Q, K, V Gradients & Adjoint Tensors)*
* **Core Text & References**: *Attention Is All You Need* (Vaswani et al.) / *Matrix Differentiation of Multi-Head Self-Attention*
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Scaled Dot-Product Attention Forward Equation**:
     * Given $Q, K, V \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_k}$, define pre-softmax logits $S \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L}$ and attention weights $P \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L}$:
       $$S = \frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}, \quad P = \operatorname{softmax}(S) \quad (\text{row-wise}), \quad O = P V$$
  2. **Upstream Adjoint Gradients through Attention**:
     * Given upstream loss gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial O} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_v}$:
       * **Gradient w.r.t. Value ($V$)**:
         $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial V} = P^T \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial O}$$
       * **Gradient w.r.t. Pre-Softmax Logits ($S$)**:
         $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial P} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial O} V^T, \quad \left[\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial S}\right]_{ij} = P_{ij} \left( \left[\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial P}\right]_{ij} - \sum_{k=1}^L \left[\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial P}\right]_{ik} P_{ik} \right)$$
       * **Gradients w.r.t. Query ($Q$) and Key ($K$)**:
         $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Q} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial S} K, \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial K} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}} \left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial S}\right)^T Q$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Derive $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial V} = P^T \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial O}$ using the trace identity: $d\mathcal{L} = \operatorname{Tr}\left(\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial O}\right)^T dO\right) = \operatorname{Tr}\left(\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial O}\right)^T P \, dV\right) = \operatorname{Tr}\left(P^T \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial O} \, dV\right)$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Q} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial S} K$ and $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial K} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}} \left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial S}\right)^T Q$ from $dS = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}(dQ \, K^T + Q \, dK^T)$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For causal autoregressive attention where $P_{ij} = 0$ for $j > i$ (masked upper triangle), verify how the sparsity mask directly zeroes out the corresponding elements in $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial S}$.
