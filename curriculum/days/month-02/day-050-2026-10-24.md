### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Topic & Mathematical Foundation**: Spectral Graph Theory: Adjacency, Degree Matrix & The Graph Laplacian
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Graph Matrices**:
     * For graph $G = (V, E)$ with $|V|=n$: Adjacency matrix $W_{ij} \in \{0, 1\}$, Degree matrix $D_{ii} = \sum_{j} W_{ij}$.
  2. **The Graph Laplacian Matrix**:
     $$L = D - W \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$$
  3. **Quadratic Form & Positive Semi-Definiteness**:
     $$\mathbf{x}^T L \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x}^T D \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}^T W \mathbf{x} = \sum_{i=1}^n d_i x_i^2 - \sum_{i,j} W_{ij} x_i x_j = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n W_{ij}(x_i - x_j)^2 \ge 0$$
     * Since $\mathbf{x}^T L \mathbf{x} \ge 0$ for all $\mathbf{x}$, $L$ is **symmetric positive semi-definite ($L \succeq 0$)**.
  4. **Algebraic Connectivity & The Fiedler Vector**:
     * Spectrum: $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_n$.
     * Multiplicity of $\lambda=0$ equals the exact number of connected components in $G$.
     * The second smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ (Fiedler value) measures graph connectivity / network robustness.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove $\mathbf{x}^T L \mathbf{x} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j} W_{ij}(x_i - x_j)^2$ by expanding $\sum_{i,j} W_{ij}(x_i^2 - 2x_i x_j + x_j^2)$ and regrouping terms with $d_i = \sum_j W_{ij}$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that $\mathbf{1} = \langle 1, 1, \dots, 1 \rangle^T$ is in the nullspace of $L$ ($L \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}$), confirming $\lambda_1 = 0$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For a 4-vertex star graph $S_4$ (center node 1 connected to leaves 2, 3, 4), construct $W, D, L$ and compute the eigenvalues of $L$.

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Topic & Mathematical Foundation**: The Hubness Phenomenon in High Dimensions, $k$-Occurrence Skewness & Covariance Whitening
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Hubness Phenomenon in $\mathbb{R}^d$**:
     * In high-dimensional spaces ($d \ge 100$), the variance of distances to the origin induces extreme asymmetry in $k$-nearest neighbor graphs.
     * Let $N_k(\mathbf{x}_i)$ be the number of times vector $\mathbf{x}_i$ appears in the $k$-NN sets of all dataset points.
     * Universal Sum Rule:
       $$\sum_{i=1}^n N_k(\mathbf{x}_i) = n \cdot k \implies \mathbb{E}[N_k] = k$$
  2. **Skewness of the $k$-Occurrence Distribution**:
     $$S_{N_k} = \frac{\mathbb{E}\left[(N_k - k)^3\right]}{\operatorname{Var}(N_k)^{3/2}} = \frac{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n (N_k(\mathbf{x}_i) - k)^3}{\left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n (N_k(\mathbf{x}_i) - k)^2 \right)^{3/2}}$$
     * Uncorrelated data has $S_{N_k} \approx 0$. High-dimensional neural embeddings exhibit $S_{N_k} > 3.0$, creating massive "hub" nodes with in-degrees $> 100 \times k$ that trap graph searches.
  3. **Covariance Whitening (Mahalanobis Isotropization)**:
     * Given covariance $\mathbf{\Sigma} = \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{\Lambda} \mathbf{Q}^T$:
       $$\mathbf{x}_{\text{white}} = \mathbf{\Lambda}^{-1/2} \mathbf{Q}^T (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}) \implies \operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{white}}) = \mathbf{I}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove $\sum_{i=1}^n N_k(\mathbf{x}_i) = nk$ by defining the indicator matrix $A_{ij} = \mathbb{I}(\mathbf{x}_j \in \text{kNN}(\mathbf{x}_i))$ and evaluating $\sum_i \sum_j A_{ij}$ across rows and columns.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that $\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{white}}) = \mathbf{I}$ by expanding $\mathbb{E}[(\mathbf{\Lambda}^{-1/2}\mathbf{Q}^T \mathbf{x}_c)(\mathbf{\Lambda}^{-1/2}\mathbf{Q}^T \mathbf{x}_c)^T]$ where $\mathbf{x}_c = \mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For a dataset of 5 points with $k=2$ neighbor occurrences $N_2 = [0, 0, 1, 1, 8]$, calculate the sample mean, variance, and exact skewness $S_{N_2}$.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Topic & Mathematical Foundation**: Spectral Graph Partitioning, The Fiedler Vector & Cheeger's Inequality
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Fiedler Eigenvalue (Algebraic Connectivity)**:
     * The second smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_2(L)$ of the Graph Laplacian $L = D - W$:
       $$\lambda_2 = \min_{\mathbf{x} \perp \mathbf{1}, \mathbf{x} \ne \mathbf{0}} \frac{\mathbf{x}^T L \mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{x}} = \min_{\sum x_i = 0} \frac{\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i,j} W_{ij}(x_i - x_j)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2}$$
     * The corresponding eigenvector $\mathbf{v}_2$ (the **Fiedler vector**) gives the optimal continuous relaxation for min-cut graph bisection.
  2. **The Cheeger Constant (Graph Conductance)**:
     $$h(G) = \min_{S \subset V, 0 < |S| \le |V|/2} \frac{e(S, V \setminus S)}{|S|}$$
  3. **Cheeger's Inequality**:
     $$\frac{\lambda_2}{2} \le h(G) \le \sqrt{2 d_{\max} \lambda_2}$$
     * Direct implication: Small $\lambda_2$ implies a tight structural bottleneck where beam searches get trapped.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove using the Courant-Fischer Min-Max Theorem that $\lambda_2 = \min_{\mathbf{x} \ne \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{1} = 0} \frac{\mathbf{x}^T L \mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{x}}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: For a dumbbell graph (two $K_4$ cliques joined by a single edge), construct the Laplacian matrix $L$ and explain why the sign of the Fiedler vector separates the two cliques.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Compute the Cheeger conductance $h(G)$ for a 6-node cycle graph $C_6$.
