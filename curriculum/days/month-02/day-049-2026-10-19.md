### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Topic**: *Computational DAGs, Topological Sorting & Automatic Differentiation Complexity*
* **Core Text & References**: *Evaluating Derivatives: Principles and Techniques of Algorithmic Differentiation* (Griewank & Walther) / *Automatic Differentiation in Machine Learning: A Survey* (Baydin et al.)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Computational Graphs & Topological Order**:
     * A neural network is represented as a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) $G = (V, E)$ where nodes $v_i \in V$ are elementary operations and edges carry intermediate tensor values.
     * Nodes are topologically sorted such that if $v_i$ depends on $v_j$, then $j < i$.
  2. **Forward-Mode AD (Jacobian-Vector Products / JVPs)**:
     * Propagates tangent vectors $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt}$ forward alongside the primal evaluation: $\dot{\mathbf{y}} = J \dot{\mathbf{x}}$.
     * For a function $f: \mathbb{R}^N \to \mathbb{R}^M$, computing the full Jacobian requires **$N$ forward passes** (1 per input dimension). Highly efficient when $N \ll M$.
  3. **Reverse-Mode AD (Vector-Jacobian Products / VJPs / Backprop)**:
     * Propagates adjoint vectors $\bar{\mathbf{y}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{y}}$ backward from scalar loss $\mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}$: $\bar{\mathbf{x}} = \bar{\mathbf{y}}^T J$.
     * For a scalar loss $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^N \to \mathbb{R}$ where $N = 100\text{ Billion}$, reverse-mode computes the exact gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ in **1 single backward pass** (time complexity $\le 3\times \text{forward pass}$).
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove the Griewank-Walther Baur-Strassen Theorem: the computational work $W(\nabla f)$ of evaluating the gradient of a scalar function $f(x_1, \dots, x_N)$ via reverse-mode AD is bounded by $W(\nabla f) \le 4 \cdot W(f)$, strictly independent of input dimension $N$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Construct the computational DAG for $f(x_1, x_2) = \ln(x_1 x_2) + \sin(x_1^2 + x_2)$. Label intermediate nodes $v_1, v_2, \dots, v_6$, write out the forward tape, and execute the reverse adjoint pass step-by-step to compute $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}$ and $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_2}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Contrast the memory consumption of Forward-Mode AD ($O(1)$ memory, no tape needed) vs. Reverse-Mode AD ($O(T)$ memory storing all intermediate activations for the backward pass). Explain how **Activation Checkpointing (Rematerialization)** trades recomputation FLOPS for $O(\sqrt{T})$ memory.

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Topic**: *Loss Surface Curvature, Hessians, Condition Numbers & Optimizer Dynamics*
* **Core Text & References**: *Convex Optimization* (Boyd & Vandenberghe §9.1–9.3) / *Optimization for Machine Learning* (Sra, Nowozin, Wright)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Second-Order Taylor Approximation & The Hessian Matrix**:
     * For loss function $\mathcal{L}: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}$, the local quadratic model around $\mathbf{w}_0$ is:
       $$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}) \approx \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}_0) + \nabla \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}_0)^T (\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{w}_0) + \frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{w}_0)^T H (\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{w}_0)$$
       where $H = \nabla^2 \mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ is the symmetric Hessian matrix.
  2. **Condition Number ($\kappa$) & Ill-Conditioned Ravines**:
     * Let $\lambda_{\max}$ and $\lambda_{\min}$ be the maximum and minimum eigenvalues of $H$.
     * Condition number $\kappa = \frac{\lambda_{\max}}{\lambda_{\min}}$. When $\kappa \gg 1$, the loss surface forms an anisotropic ravine (steep in one direction, flat in another).
     * Standard Gradient Descent with step size $\eta$ oscillates wildly unless $\eta < \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}}$, resulting in agonizingly slow progress along the flat direction with convergence rate $\mathcal{O}\left( \left(\frac{\kappa - 1}{\kappa + 1}\right)^2 \right)$.
  3. **Momentum and AdamW Preconditioning**:
     * **Polyak Heavy-Ball Momentum**: Accelerates traversal along low-curvature directions, improving the convergence rate to $\mathcal{O}\left( \left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa} - 1}{\sqrt{\kappa} + 1}\right)^2 \right)$.
     * **AdamW Adaptive Second Moments ($v_t$)**: Re-scales gradient coordinates by $1 / \sqrt{v_t + \epsilon} \approx 1 / \sqrt{\operatorname{diag}(H)}$, acting as an efficient diagonal approximation to Newton's method ($H^{-1} \nabla \mathcal{L}$) without computing or inverting the $D \times D$ Hessian.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: For quadratic loss $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}) = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{w}^T H \mathbf{w}$, show that gradient descent iteration $\mathbf{w}_{k+1} = (I - \eta H)\mathbf{w}_k$ converges to $\mathbf{0}$ if and only if $\rho(I - \eta H) < 1$, proving that the optimal learning rate is $\eta^* = \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max} + \lambda_{\min}}$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Prove that with the optimal step size $\eta^*$, the contraction factor of gradient descent is $\frac{\lambda_{\max} - \lambda_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max} + \lambda_{\min}} = \frac{\kappa - 1}{\kappa + 1}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For loss $\mathcal{L}(w_1, w_2) = 100 w_1^2 + w_2^2$, compute the Hessian $H$, find its eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ and condition number $\kappa$, and compute the exact trajectory of 3 gradient descent steps with $\eta = 0.009$.
