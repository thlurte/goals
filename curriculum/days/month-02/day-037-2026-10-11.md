### 🔹 Part 1: Advanced Theorems & Derivations (Thursday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Sections**: §14.3–14.4 (*Double Integrals in Polar Coordinates & The Gaussian Integral*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **The Polar Area Element Jacobian**:
     $$dA = dx dy = \left| \det \mathbf{J} \right| dr d\theta = \left| \det \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -r\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & r\cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \right| dr d\theta = r \, dr \, d\theta$$
  2. **The Gaussian Integral Proof**:
     $$I = \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-x^2} dx \implies I^2 = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-(x^2 + y^2)} dx dy$$
     * Converting to polar coordinates ($x^2 + y^2 = r^2, dx dy = r dr d\theta$):
       $$I^2 = \int_{\theta=0}^{2\pi} d\theta \int_{r=0}^\infty e^{-r^2} r dr = 2\pi \left[ -\frac{1}{2} e^{-r^2} \right]_0^\infty = \pi \implies I = \sqrt{\pi}$$
  3. **Normalizing the Gaussian Probability Distribution**:
     $$\int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{x^2}{2\sigma^2}} dx = 1$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Compute the $2 \times 2$ Jacobian determinant for the Cartesian-to-Polar transformation to prove $dA = r \, dr \, d\theta$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Write out the complete double integration proof of the Gaussian integral $\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-x^2} dx = \sqrt{\pi}$ without skipping any substitution steps.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Evaluate $\iint_D (x^2 + y^2) dA$ over the disk $D = \{(x, y) \mid x^2 + y^2 \le a^2\}$ using polar coordinates.

### 🔹 Part 2: Core Proofs & Problem Sets (Friday Syllabus)
* **Book**: *Calculus* — Gilbert Strang
* **Section**: §14.7 (*Change of Variables in Multiple Integrals & The Jacobian Determinant*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Change of Variables in $\mathbb{R}^n$**:
     $$\int \dots \int_R f(\mathbf{x}) d\mathbf{x} = \int \dots \int_S f(T(\mathbf{u})) \left| \det \mathbf{J}_T(\mathbf{u}) \right| d\mathbf{u}$$
  2. **The 3D Spherical Coordinate Jacobian**:
     $$\mathbf{J} = \begin{pmatrix} \sin\phi\cos\theta & \rho\cos\phi\cos\theta & -\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta \\ \sin\phi\sin\theta & \rho\cos\phi\sin\theta & \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta \\ \cos\phi & -\rho\sin\phi & 0 \end{pmatrix} \implies \left| \det \mathbf{J} \right| = \rho^2 \sin\phi$$
     $$dV = dx dy dz = \rho^2 \sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$$
  3. **Linear Coordinate Transformations**:
     * For linear map $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}$, the Jacobian is constant: $\left|\det \mathbf{J}\right| = |\det \mathbf{A}|$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Expand the $3 \times 3$ determinant of the Spherical Coordinate transformation matrix step-by-step along the third row to prove $|\det \mathbf{J}| = \rho^2 \sin\phi$.
  - [ ] **Problem 2**: Use the transformation $u = x + y, v = x - 2y$ to evaluate $\iint_R (x + y)^2 dx dy$ over the parallelogram bounded by $x+y=0, x+y=2, x-2y=0, x-2y=3$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: Compute the volume of an ellipsoid $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} + \frac{z^2}{c^2} \le 1$ by applying scaling map $x=au, y=bv, z=cw$ and integrating over the unit sphere.
