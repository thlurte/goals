## 🌅 06:00 – 07:30 | Pure Mathematics (90 min)
* **Topic**: *Matrix Calculus & Neural Network Backpropagation Foundations*
* **Core Text & References**: *Matrix Calculus for Deep Learning* (Parr & Howard) / *The Matrix Cookbook* (Petersen & Pedersen)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Matrix Derivative Definitions & Layout**:
     * For scalar loss $\mathcal{L} \in \mathbb{R}$ and matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$, the gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$ is defined element-wise by $\left[\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W}\right]_{ij} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_{ij}}$.
  2. **Differential & Trace Identities**:
     * $d\mathcal{L} = \operatorname{Tr}\left(\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W}\right)^T dW\right)$.
     * Cyclic property of trace: $\operatorname{Tr}(ABC) = \operatorname{Tr}(CAB) = \operatorname{Tr}(BCA)$.
     * Frobenius inner product: $\langle A, B \rangle = \operatorname{Tr}(A^T B)$.
  3. **Analytical Backprop for Batched Linear Layer ($Y = XW + b$)**:
     * Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times D_{\text{in}}}$, $W \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\text{in}} \times D_{\text{out}}}$, and $b \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times D_{\text{out}}}$. Given upstream gradient $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times D_{\text{out}}}$:
       $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W} = X^T \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y}, \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial X} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y} W^T, \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial b} = \mathbf{1}_{B}^T \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial Y}$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:

## 📖 07:30 – 08:30 | Systems & Architecture Reading (60 min)
* **Paper**: *"Accelerating Large-Scale Inference with Anisotropic Vector Quantization"* (Guo et al., ICML 2020 / Google Research ScaNN) §1–3
* **Core Microarchitectural Concepts**:
  1. **Anisotropic Error Decomposition in MIPS**:
     * Let $\mathbf{x}$ be a database vector and $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ its quantized approximation. Error $\mathbf{e} = \mathbf{x} - \tilde{\mathbf{x}}$.
     * Split $\mathbf{e}$ into parallel component $\mathbf{e}_\parallel = \frac{\langle \mathbf{e}, \mathbf{x} \rangle}{\|\mathbf{x}\|_2^2} \mathbf{x}$ and orthogonal component $\mathbf{e}_\perp = \mathbf{e} - \mathbf{e}_\parallel$.
     * Inner product error: $\langle \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{x} \rangle - \langle \mathbf{q}, \tilde{\mathbf{x}} \rangle = \langle \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{e}_\parallel \rangle + \langle \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{e}_\perp \rangle$.
  2. **Why Parallel Error Destroys Retrieval Recall**:
     * In high dimensions, random queries are nearly orthogonal to $\mathbf{e}_\perp$, so $\mathbb{E}[\langle \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{e}_\perp \rangle] \approx 0$.
     * However, $\mathbf{e}_\parallel$ directly shrinks or expands the effective length $\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\|$, systematically destroying top-ranked inner product candidates.
  3. **The ScaNN Loss Function**:
     $$\ell_{\text{aniso}}(\mathbf{x}, \tilde{\mathbf{x}}) = h \|\mathbf{e}_\parallel\|_2^2 + 1.0 \|\mathbf{e}_\perp\|_2^2 \quad \text{where } h \ge 5.0$$

     ) {
         float x_norm_sq = 0.0f;
         float dot_xe = 0.0f;
         float total_e_sq = 0.0f;

         for (size_t d = 0; d < dim; ++d) {
             float diff = x[d] - c[d];
             x_norm_sq += x[d] * x[d];
             dot_xe += x[d] * diff;
             total_e_sq += diff * diff;
