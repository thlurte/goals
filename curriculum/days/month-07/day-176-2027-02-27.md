### 🔹 Part 1: Theory & Concepts (Monday Syllabus)
* **Book / Paper**: *Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective (CS:APP)* Ch 9 & Woosuk Kwon et al. *vLLM: Efficient Memory Management for Large Language Models* (2023) §3–4
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Memory Fragmentation in Contiguous vs Paged Caches**:
     * **Contiguous Static Allocation**: Pre-allocates maximum sequence length $L_{\max}$.
       $$\text{Internal Waste } W_{\text{cont}} = \frac{L_{\max} - L_{\text{actual}}}{L_{\max}}$$
       Under uniformly distributed sequence lengths $L \sim \text{Uniform}(1, L_{\max})$, expected waste is $\mathbb{E}[W_{\text{cont}}] \approx 50\%$.
     * **Paged Allocation (Block Size $B$)**:
       $$\text{Internal Waste } W_{\text{paged}} = \frac{L_{\text{actual}} \bmod B}{L_{\text{actual}}} \le \frac{B}{L_{\text{actual}}}$$
       For $B = 16, \bar{L} = 2048$, expected waste is $\frac{8}{2048} \approx 0.39\%$ ($>99\%$ memory utilization).
  2. **Virtual Memory Address Translation Mapping**:
     * Logical Token $t \in [0, L-1]$:
       $$b_{\text{logical}} = \lfloor t / B \rfloor, \quad \text{offset } o = t \bmod B$$
       $$b_{\text{physical}} = \operatorname{BlockTable}[\text{req\_id}][b_{\text{logical}}]$$
       $$\text{Physical Address} = \text{K\_Pool} + b_{\text{physical}} \cdot (B \cdot H_{kv} \cdot d \cdot 2) + o \cdot (H_{kv} \cdot d \cdot 2)$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove that under power-law sequence lengths $P(L) \propto L^{-\alpha}$, contiguous pre-allocation waste exceeds $70\%$, whereas paged allocation maintains waste below $\frac{B}{\mathbb{E}[L]} < 2\%$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the maximum concurrency ratio: Show that maximum concurrent serving capacity scales as $\frac{B_{\max}^{\text{paged}}}{B_{\max}^{\text{cont}}} = \frac{L_{\max}}{\mathbb{E}[L]}$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For a 70B parameter model with $24\text{ GB}$ dedicated KV cache VRAM ($S_{\text{token}} = 128\text{ KB}$ per token) with $L_{\max} = 8192$ and mean length $\bar{L} = 1024$: calculate max active concurrent requests for (a) Contiguous buffer ($24$ requests) vs (b) Paged KV cache ($192$ requests, $8\times$ throughput increase).

### 🔹 Part 2: Properties & Geometric Symmetries (Tuesday Syllabus)
* **Book / Paper**: *Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss & TurboQuant: Rate-Distortion Bounds for Attention Key-Value Caching* — Amir Zandieh et al. (2024)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Orthogonal Dimensions of KV-Cache Compression**:
     * Total Memory Footprint:
       $$\text{Memory} = B_{\text{active}} \times L \times 2 H_{kv} \times d \times b \text{ bits}$$
     * **Paging (vLLM)**: Optimizes allocation geometry ($B_{\text{active}} \times L$), eliminating fragmentation $W \to 0$.
     * **Quantization (TurboQuant)**: Optimizes representation bitwidth ($b: 16 \to 3$ bits).
     * **Multiplicative Scaling Theorem**:
       $$\text{Total VRAM Savings} = S_{\text{paging}} \times S_{\text{quant}} = \left(\frac{L_{\max}}{\mathbb{E}[L]}\right) \times \left(\frac{16}{b}\right) \approx 4 \times 5.33 \approx 21.3\times$$
  2. **Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss (QJL) Unbiased Estimator**:
     * Under random orthogonal rotation $\Pi \in \mathcal{O}(d)$ and $b$-bit scalar quantizer $\mathcal{Q}_b$:
       $$\mathbb{E}_{\Pi}\left[ \langle \mathbf{q}, \Pi^T \mathcal{Q}_b(\Pi \mathbf{k}) \rangle \right] = \langle \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{k} \rangle$$
       $$\operatorname{Var}\left( \langle \mathbf{q}, \Pi^T \mathcal{Q}_b(\Pi \mathbf{k}) \rangle \right) \le \frac{C_b}{d} \|\mathbf{q}\|_2^2 \|\mathbf{k}\|_2^2 \quad \left(C_b \approx \frac{\pi}{2} 2^{-2b}\right)$$
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove the QJL unbiasedness theorem: Show that $\mathbb{E}[\Pi^T \mathcal{Q}_b(\Pi \mathbf{k})] = \mathbf{k}$ over the Haar measure on orthogonal matrices.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the variance decay $\mathcal{O}(1/d)$: Show that random orthogonal mixing disperses outlier coordinates across all $d$ dimensions, bounding maximum quantization error.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For a $128\text{k}$ context request on a 70B model ($128\text{ GB}$ baseline FP16 contiguous KV cache): calculate memory footprint under combined PagedAttention ($4\times$) + 3-bit PolarQuant ($5.33\times$), demonstrating total reduction to $6.0\text{ GB}$.

### 🔹 Part 3: Core Theorems & Derivations (Wednesday Syllabus)
* **Book / Topic**: *Online Computation and Competitive Analysis* — Allan Borodin & Ran El-Yaniv Ch 3 (*The Paging Problem & Sleator-Tarjan Theorem*)
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Competitive Ratio of Online Paging**:
     * For fast GPU cache capacity $k$ physical blocks and slow Host RAM:
       $$\operatorname{Cost}_{\text{LRU}}(\sigma) \le k \cdot \operatorname{Cost}_{\text{OPT}}(\sigma) + \mathcal{O}(k)$$
     * **Sleator-Tarjan Lower Bound**: No deterministic online caching algorithm can achieve a competitive ratio strictly less than $k$.
  2. **Effective Tiered Access Latency Model**:
     * Let $h$ be the GPU VRAM page hit rate, $T_{\text{VRAM}}$ be VRAM read latency, and $T_{\text{PCIe}}$ be PCIe migration latency:
       $$T_{\text{eff}} = h \cdot T_{\text{VRAM}} + (1 - h) \cdot (T_{\text{PCIe}} + T_{\text{VRAM}})$$
     * With attention power-law locality ($P(\text{access}) \propto \Delta t^{-0.8}$), empirical hit rate $h \ge 0.93 \implies T_{\text{eff}} \le 1.12 \cdot T_{\text{VRAM}}$.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove the Sleator-Tarjan theorem: Partition request sequence $\sigma$ into maximal phases containing at most $k$ distinct pages, and show LRU incurs at most $k$ faults per phase while OPT must incur at least 1 fault.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the condition under which asynchronous page prefetching completely hides PCIe transfer latency: $T_{\text{compute}}(\text{Token}_{t}) \ge T_{\text{PCIe}}(\text{Page}_{t+1})$.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For PCIe 5.0 $\times 16$ with effective bidirectional bandwidth $58\text{ GB/s}$, compute the transfer time for a 16-token physical block ($32\text{ KB}$): $t_{\text{xfer}} = \frac{32 \times 10^3}{58 \times 10^9} = 0.55\ \mu\text{s}$, verifying that transfer finishes within the single-token decode time ($\approx 8\text{ ms}$).
