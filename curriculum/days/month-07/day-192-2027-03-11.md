## 🌅 06:00 – 07:30 | Pure Mathematics (90 min)
* **Book / Topic**: *Hybrid Retrieval Theory: Reciprocal Rank Fusion & Convex Score Spaces* — Gordon V. Cormack et al.
* **Theorems & Mathematical Concepts**:
  1. **Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) Formulation**:
     * Given $M$ independent retrieval rankings (e.g. Dense HNSW, Multi-Vector MaxSim, Sparse BM25):
       $$\operatorname{Score}_{\text{RRF}}(d) = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{1}{k_0 + r_m(d)}$$
     * Where $r_m(d) \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}$ is the rank of document $d$ in system $m$, and $k_0 \approx 60$ is the rank damping constant.
  2. **Monotonic Invariance Theorem**:
     * **Theorem**: For any strictly monotonic score transformations $g_m: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $g_m'(s) > 0$, the RRF score is strictly invariant:
       $$\operatorname{RRF}(\{g_m(S_m)\}_{m=1}^M) \equiv \operatorname{RRF}(\{S_m\}_{m=1}^M)$$
     * Completely eliminates cross-model calibration and score distribution mismatch between uncalibrated cosine logits and BM25 scores.
* **Pencil & Paper Problem Set**:
  - [ ] **Proof 1**: Prove the RRF Monotonic Invariance Theorem by showing that ranking permutations $\pi_m = \operatorname{argsort}(S_m)$ are invariant under any strictly increasing bijection $g_m$.
  - [ ] **Proof 2**: Derive the maximum score bound $\operatorname{Score}_{\text{RRF}}(d) \le \frac{M}{k_0 + 1}$ and prove that agreeing on rank 1 across all $M$ systems produces the unique global maximum.
  - [ ] **Problem 3**: For a hybrid search query with Dense rank $r_{\text{dense}}(d_A) = 1, r_{\text{dense}}(d_B) = 3$ and BM25 rank $r_{\text{BM25}}(d_A) = 5, r_{\text{BM25}}(d_B) = 1$, compute RRF scores with $k_0 = 60$ and determine whether document $A$ or $B$ ranks first.
